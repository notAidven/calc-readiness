# Cross-domain prose QA — Stream E

Read-only sweep of all 62 packs plus the shared copy surfaces, at `3fbe5fcb` on
`anthony/all-domain-qa`. Rules cited by number from `.cursor/skills/author/SKILL.md`.

Nothing in this report was edited or fixed. Findings that did not survive scoping are listed
as rejected, with the reason, rather than dropped silently.

## Method, and what it can and cannot prove

The census reads the prose of every pack in **era order** (`packages/skill-graph/src/eras.ts`),
which is play order and is not domain order: `RAT.5` (d5) precedes `FUN.2` (d6), and `FUN.10`
(d6) precedes `LF.1` (d7). Three of the seven findings below exist only because of that
re-ordering, and no per-domain round could have seen them.

For each term I recorded first use and first **gloss**, taking a gloss to be a bold span equal
to the term (allowing plurals), since that is how these packs mark an introduction. Counting
alone produced false positives at a high rate, so every finding here was then read in context;
two candidates died on reading and are recorded as cleared. Treat the mechanical half as a
triage that ranks candidates, not as evidence.

### 1.1 `range` is taught with two incompatible meanings, and the collision is exact — R32

`content/lessons/d3/LIN.4.json` `mini_lesson[2]` (era 3, d3) uses the word three times for the
set of **x-values** an inequality allows:

> "**interval notation** is the standard short form: the two ends of the range in order […]
> whether one is **closed** (inside the range) or **open** (outside it) […] A range with no
> limit on one side carries the infinity symbol"

with `subtitle[2]` "Writing a range down" and `we[2].steps[3]` "Nothing limits the range above".

Four eras later `content/lessons/d6/FUN.2.json` `mini_lesson[0]` glosses it as the opposite axis:

> "the **domain** is every input the rule accepts, and the **range** is every output the rule
> can reach"

This is worse than an ordinary early-use case because both packs pair the word with the **same
notation**. `FUN.2`'s `cfu.prompt` asks the learner to "Give its **range** (every output the
rule can reach) in interval notation" — and interval notation is precisely what `LIN.4` taught
while calling its contents "the range". A learner meets "the range, written in interval
notation" twice, meaning x-values the first time and y-values the second.

**A learner would wrongly conclude** that `FUN.2` is asking for the inputs, and would answer
`FUN.2`'s CFU with the domain. `FUN.2` `mini_lesson[0]` even names that error — "Reading one
off when the question asked for the other is the easiest mistake to make here" — without
knowing that its own curriculum trained it four eras earlier.

### 1.2 `coefficient` is used bare in era 1 and then introduced three times — R32

`content/lessons/d1/NUM.4.json` (era 1, the learner's first domain) never defines the word but
builds a slide on it. `subtitle[1]` is "Adding the coefficients", `mini_lesson[1]` is "To
combine like terms, **add the coefficients** and keep the variable part" — the bold is on the
instruction, not the term — and `we[0].steps[1]` assumes it outright: "$9b$ carries the variable
part $b$, and so does $b$, whose coefficient of $1$ is written by leaving it off."

It is then introduced as new three separate times, in three domains, and two of the three are
not the same definition:

- `content/lessons/d2/RAD.1.json` `mini_lesson[3]` (era 2): "the number standing in front is
  called the **coefficient**" — scoped to what sits in front of a radical.
- `content/lessons/d3/LIN.1.json` `mini_lesson[1]` (era 3): "the number multiplying the variable
  is its **coefficient**".
- `content/lessons/d4/POL.1.json` `mini_lesson[0]` (era 4): "the number in front of a term is
  its **coefficient**", repeated in `we[0].steps[1]` as "A coefficient is the number in front of
  a term".

**A learner would wrongly conclude**, at `NUM.4`, that "coefficient" is a word they were
supposed to already have; and on reaching `RAD.1` that it is a fact about radicals specifically,
since that gloss says nothing about multiplying a variable. `RAD.1`'s definition does not
survive contact with `LIN.1`'s.

### 1.3 `set-builder` notation is required three eras before it is explained — R32

`content/lessons/d5/RAT.5.json` `mini_lesson[0]` (era 6) hands over both the name and the
symbols with no account of either:

> "the **domain** is the set of every other input, written in set-builder form as
> $\{x \mid x \ne c\}$"

and then `we[0].steps[3]` writes an answer in it: "In set-builder form the domain is
$\{x \mid x \ne -6\}$."

The braces and the bar are explained only in `content/lessons/d8/EL.4.json` `mini_lesson[3]`
(era 9), three eras and two domains later:

> "**Set-builder notation** writes the two cases as $\{x \mid x > h\}$ and $\{x \mid x < h\}$,
> where the braces say the collection of all values of $x$ for which the condition after the
> bar holds."

Nothing before `RAT.5` introduces it; `LIN.4` (era 3) teaches interval notation, which is a
different notation.

**A learner would wrongly conclude** that `\mid` is a division or absolute-value bar — both of
which they have met and this one is not — and would read $\{x \mid x \ne -6\}$ as an expression
to evaluate rather than as a set description. `RAT.5`'s CFU does not require typing it, so the
cost is comprehension of the worked example rather than a grading dead end.

### 1.4 `function`, `input` and `output` are used technically two eras before `FUN.1` — R32

`content/lessons/d5/RAT.1.json` (era 5) leans on the precise sense of all three. `mini_lesson[3]`
turns on it entirely:

> "a rational expression is not the same function as its reduced form until the restrictions
> travel with it"

and `mini_lesson[2]`: "A simplified expression and the one it came from can therefore disagree
at exactly one input".

They are glossed in `content/lessons/d6/FUN.1.json` `mini_lesson[0]` (era 7): "A **function**
is exactly that kind of rule: it takes an **input** and returns exactly one **output**."

**A learner would wrongly conclude** nothing at all from `RAT.1`'s central claim, which is the
problem: "not the same function" is doing the whole work of the slide, and "function" is the
word they have not been given. The fix is cheap and that is what makes this a finding rather
than a nit — `RAT.1` could say "disagree at exactly one value of $x$" and lose nothing.

### 1.5 Three terms are introduced as new in three different domains

Not wrong, but each spends a slide opening on ground already held, and no per-domain round
could see the repetition.

- **`absolute value`**, four times: `NUM.S` `mini_lesson[0]` (e1) "**Absolute value** throws the
  direction away and keeps only the distance from zero"; `RAD.4` `mini_lesson[1]` (e2), which
  earns its re-introduction by adding the $|x|$ notation; then `LIN.5` `mini_lesson[0]` (e3)
  "which is what the **absolute value** of a number is: its distance from zero, written between
  two upright bars" and `FUN.10` `mini_lesson[0]` (e8) "**Absolute value** is that rule written
  down". The last two restate concept and notation together, adding nothing over `RAD.4`.
- **`domain`**, three times: `RAT.5` (e6), `FUN.2` (e7), `EL.4` (e9) "The set of inputs a
  function accepts is its **domain**". Note the ordering: the canonical introduction in the
  functions pack arrives **second**, because `RAT.5` gets there an era earlier in play.
- **`vertical asymptote`**, three times: `RAT.5` (e6), `EL.4` (e9), and `TRG.6` (e10)
  "A **vertical asymptote** is a heading like that one: the function has no value there at all",
  a from-scratch definition four eras after the first.

### 1.6 Checked and cleared

Recorded because the mechanical pass flagged both and reading cleared both.

- **`parity`** — flagged NEVER-GLOSSED (4 uses, `POL.6`/`POL.7`, no bold anywhere). Rejected:
  `POL.6` `we[2].steps[1]` glosses it inline — "whether a number is odd or even is its parity,
  which is the whole of what this question turns on" — and that worked example is on slide 2,
  ahead of the bare use at `mini_lesson[3]` and well ahead of `POL.7`. Earned, just not bolded.
- **`bench mark`** (R23) — `CG.1` `mini_lesson[0]`, 10 uses, bolded as two words. Not a coinage:
  it is the standard surveying term, and the same sentence maps it to the **origin**.

No R23 coinage was found. Every flagged term exists outside this repo.

## 2. R34 — section 3 replaying section 2

### 2.1 Confirmed replays

Verified by reading each widget's board data against the worked example on the same slide, not
from a report. Slide index is the pack's `visuals[i]` position; the widget's **first** board is
the one a learner gets on arrival.

| Pack | Slide | Widget | Worked example directly above | Widget board 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `EXP.1` d2 | 5 | `NegativeExponentEstimate` | $2^{-1}=\tfrac12$, then $2^{-2}=\tfrac14$ | `{base:2,exponent:-1,value:0.5}`, `{base:2,exponent:-2,value:0.25}` |
| `RAD.3` d2 | 3 | `RadicalFormBuilder` | "Write $x^{4/3}$ in radical form." | `POOLS[0][0] = {p:4,q:3}` |
| `RAD.3` d2 | 4 | `ExponentFormBuilder` | "Write $\sqrt[3]{x^{5}}$ with a single fraction exponent." | `POOLS[0][0] = {p:5,q:3}` |
| `EL.2` d8 | 2 | `ExpTransformBuilder` | "Reflect $y = 5 \cdot 3^{x}$ across the $x$-axis" | `{a:5,b:3,move:"reflected over the $x$-axis"}` |
| `EL.3` d8 | 1 | `LogFormMatch` | `we.step[1]` "$2^{5} = 32$" from $\log_2 32 = 5$ | `pairs[0] = {left:"2^{5} = 32", right:"\\log_{2} 32 = 5"}` |

`EXP.1` slide 5 is the worst of the five: **both** rounds of board 0 replay the worked example,
in the same order, so the whole board is answered by the text above it.

**A learner would wrongly conclude** that they have demonstrated the skill. The gate opens on a
board whose answers are printed on the same slide, so it measures reading rather than the
procedure, and a learner who has not understood the rule clears it exactly as fast as one who has.

**The reason these shipped is visible in the source, and it is one mistake made five times: the
authors checked the widget against the CFU, not against the worked example.**
`ExpTransformBuilder.tsx:56` states it outright — *"None reuses the CFU's rule, and the three
differ in the coefficient, the base and the move"* — and board 0 is the worked example's
coefficient, base and move. `EquationSameBaseStepper.tsx:59` says *"none of them the CFU's
numbers"*; `ExponentFormBuilder.tsx:72` defends discrimination (*"the p/q call cannot be settled
by size alone"*), a different property again. Each comment is true and none is about R34's
surface.

### 2.2 Partial

- **`EL.6` d8 slide 0, `EquationSameBaseStepper`.** Worked example solves $2^{t} = 32$ via
  $32 = 2^{5}$. Board 0 is `{base:2, shift:1, target:32, power:5}` — the same base, target and
  power, with a shift added. The shift is genuine new work, but the one insight the worked
  example supplies, that $32 = 2^{5}$, is handed over. Weaker than the five above; reported for
  completeness, not as a P1.

### 2.3 Named instances that did NOT hold

- **`GeoCisternPlay` board 0 against `GEO.2`** — **not a replay.** `GEO.2` slide 2's worked
  example does a tank "$10$ meters by $4$ meters and $2$ meters deep"; board 0 is
  `{kind:"prism", depth:3, length:10, width:4}`. The cross section is reused but the depth is
  not, so the volume is 120 rather than 80 and the multiplication is new. Board 1 likewise moves
  the cylinder's radius from 2 to 3. Cleared.
- **The "six D2 widgets" count is three, not six.** I checked every d2 slide carrying a gated
  widget — all 31 of them across `EXP.1`, `RAD.1`, `RAD.2`, `RAD.3`, `RAD.4` — and only the
  three tabulated above replay their worked example. The ones most likely to (`RAD.4` slide 2
  `RootParityBuilder`, `RAD.4` slide 3 `VariableRadicandBuilder`, `RAD.1` slide 3
  `RadicalFactorBuilder`, `EXP.1` slide 3 `PowerOfPowerMatch`) all use different instances:
  e.g. `RAD.4` slide 3 works $\sqrt{x^{8}}$ and $\sqrt{x^{9}}$ while the widget deals
  `{power:7}` and `{power:5}` on a different symbol.

### 2.4 Tree-wide sweep beyond the named list

I scored every pack slide that carries a gated widget by numeric overlap between the widget's
first board and the worked example above it, then read down the ranking. The top four
(`POL.5` slide 3, `POL.7` slide 2, `TRG.2` slide 2, `EL.6` slide 3) are all clean — `POL.5`
works $(x-1)(x-5)$ against boards $(x+2)(x-6)$ and $2(x+5)(x+1)$; `EL.6` slide 3 solves
$\log x + \log(x-3) = 1$ against a board on $\log x + \log(x-15) = 2$ — and the signal was dead
by that point, small integers colliding by chance.

**This is a triage, not a proof of absence.** The five confirmed replays were found by exact
instance identity, and a heuristic over integers cannot rule out a replay whose instance is
symbolic. Read §2.1 as complete for the named list and for d2, and §2.4 as "nothing further
surfaced at the top of a ranked sweep".

### 2.5 `worked_examples[i].prompt` against R34's bar

**All 264 prompt slots pass.** Zero prompts exceed two sentences (measured after stripping
`$…$` math, so a decimal point cannot be read as a full stop). The shape is consistent and
correct: each names its instance and stops — "Write $x^{4/3}$ in radical form.", "Split
$\sqrt{72}$ across the factors $4$ and $18$.", "Give the greatest number of turning points the
graph of $f(x) = (x + 3)^{2}(x - 6)^{4}$ can have." None gives the result away.

### 2.6 The `null` entries: **8, not 13**

Measured over all 62 packs: **264 `worked_examples` slots, 8 null.** The figure of 13 in the
census is wrong at this SHA. Each of the 8, argued:

| Pack | Slide | Subtitle | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `NUM.2` d1 | 0 | The four tiers | **Defensible.** Names the PEMDAS tiers; there is no instance to work, and `pemdas-key-explore` is the exercise. |
| `NUM.F` d1 | 0 | Parts of a fraction | **Defensible.** Pure naming of numerator/denominator. |
| `RAT.4` d5 | 0 | Complex fractions | **Defensible.** Defines the object before anything is done to it. |
| `FUN.1` d6 | 2 | The vertical-line test | **Defensible.** The test is visual; the slider *is* the demonstration. |
| `FUN.3` d6 | 0 | One rule per stretch | **Defensible.** Defines piecewise notation, no computation. |
| `FUN.3` d6 | 2 | Who owns the boundary | **Defensible.** States the open/closed dot rule. |
| `FUN.8` d6 | 2 | One to one | **Defensible.** Defines one-to-one and the horizontal-line test. |
| `FUN.6` d6 | 3 | Does the order matter? | **Flagged, below.** |

Seven of the eight share one honest shape: the slide **defines** something, so there is no
instance for a worked example to work. `FUN.6` slide 3 is the one that does not fit, and the
null is a symptom rather than the defect:

> "Each line of this computation rests on the one above it, so the order is forced rather than a
> matter of taste. A line written before the one it depends on still looks like algebra, which
> is what makes that mistake hard to catch. Getting the order right is most of the skill here,
> and it is the same order for every polynomial rule."
> — `content/lessons/d6/FUN.6.json` `mini_lesson[3]`

Unlike the other seven this slide defines nothing and contains no mathematics — not a symbol,
not an instance, not a named computation. It asserts that ordering "is most of the skill here"
and never shows the order, and "this computation" has no referent on the slide. **A learner
would wrongly conclude** that they have missed something, since they are told which skill
matters most and shown no instance of it. The widget carries the whole slide, so the `null` is
consistent; the prose is what is empty.

## 3. Shared copy surfaces

I read `answerFormat.ts`, `unreadableAnswer.ts`, `cfuVariants.ts`, `widgetFeedback.tsx`,
`workedSolutionVisuals.tsx`, `apps/web/src/lessons/worked/`, `practiceNotes.ts` and the seven
shared kinds in `interactive/kinds/`.

**No finding survived scoping.** Three candidates were raised and all three were rejected:

- `answerFormat.ts:129` `EXACT_TRIG_FORM_NOTE` — "Check the form: give an exact value rather
  than a decimal, take every perfect square out from under the root, and leave no root in a
  denominator." Filed as R32 for "perfect square" and rationalising. **Rejected:** it serves
  `TRG.2/3/4/6/9` (era 10) and both ideas are glossed in d2 (era 2) — perfect squares in `RAD.1`,
  clearing a root off a denominator in `RAD.2`. Earned by eight eras.
- `workedSolutionVisuals.tsx:3487` and `:3555` — captions ending "coefficient $j$, radicand $m$,
  index $2$". Filed as R32 for stacking three technical terms. **Rejected:** the registry keys
  are `d2.rad1.build.l3` and `d2.rad2.build.l3`, so these captions are reachable only from
  `RAD.1`/`RAD.2` practice, and `RAD.1` `mini_lesson[0]` and `mini_lesson[3]` gloss all three
  before that practice exists.

On R33 scope, the shared kinds' miss copy is correctly built: it names what is wrong with the
learner's specific action rather than restating the lesson. Condemning it would also condemn
hints and per-part notes, which the brief rightly excludes.

### 4.1 The finding: a development fixture is on the learner's main menu — R32

`apps/web/src/CheckerLab.tsx` is reachable by a learner from the city ☰ Menu, in the "Other
places" nav, as a peer of Diagnostic and Lessons. What it renders:

> `:17` "03"
> `:18` "Live capability probe"
> `:19` "Reduce 6/8 to lowest terms."
> `:21` "This development fixture checks exact value, fraction representation, and
> simplification as separate constraints."
> `:25` "{n} golden cases"  ·  `:26` "Node + Chromium parity"

with the overlay chrome from `App.tsx:273` — eyebrow "Capability probe", title "The D1 answer
checker" — and the menu chip labelled "Checker Lab" at `city/CitySurface.tsx:5483`.

**Reachability, since that is the whole claim.** `App.tsx:1328` passes `onOpenSurface={openSurface}`
**unconditionally**, while the `onDev*` props on lines 1326-1327 immediately above it are gated
on `devControlsEnabled`. `EntryLauncher` (`CitySurface.tsx:5477`) renders the whole nav on that
one prop being present. `winterEntryRoute.test.tsx:55-58` states the same conclusion from the
other side: *"`onOpenSurface` is passed because `App.tsx` passes it […] the winter chip is
exactly as available as Diagnostic, Lessons and Checker are — no more and no less. Rendering
without it would be testing a configuration the app never ships."*

**What a learner would wrongly conclude.** That "Checker Lab" is a place to study, since it sits
beside Lessons in the same nav with the same chip styling; and then, reading it, that "golden
cases", "Node + Chromium parity", "capability probe" and "D1" are vocabulary they have failed to
acquire. "D1" is the authoring lane's internal domain number and appears nowhere in any of the
62 packs. R32 at its strongest, because these terms are not merely unearned but un-earnable:
none of them is about mathematics, and no downstream lesson will ever define them. The surface
also states its own status in learner-visible copy — "This development fixture" — which tells
the learner the thing they just opened is not for them.

The single piece of mathematics on it, "Reduce 6/8 to lowest terms.", is one hardcoded `NUM.F`
spike item with no lesson, no hint and no progress recorded, so a learner who works it gets
nothing.

**Evidence limits, stated.** This is a reachability claim established from the tree — an
unconditional prop, a nav gated only on that prop, and a test asserting the same wiring — not
from a rendered frame. I did not screenshot the menu. Per `qa-verification.mdc` a claim about
what is visible owes a frame, so treat "a learner can open this" as strongly evidenced and
"here is what it looks like when they do" as **not framed**.

### 4.2 Hazard, not a live defect

`math-input.tsx:57` still returns `"Not quite. Check your work and try again."` on its default
branch. `answerFormat.ts:118-126` documents this exact copy as the defect the lessons lane wrote
`EXACT_TRIG_FORM_NOTE` to fix: *"Told only 'Not quite', a learner who had that value exactly
right had nothing to work with, since the arithmetic they would go back and redo was the part
that was correct."*

I am **not** filing it as an R33 violation. `feedbackFor` branches on `reason_code` and those
branches are good — `:38` "The size is right. Check the sign.", `:44` "Check the required unit
and write it after the value." The generic line is the `default`, reached when the checker does
not know why the answer was wrong, and in that state "check your work" is the honest floor.
Condemning it would condemn every fallback. Recorded as a hazard: `MathInput` is one import from
the lesson surfaces, and anything that re-mounts it inherits copy the lessons tree has already
moved past.

## Summary

- **7 cross-domain term defects**, of which §1.1 (`range` taught with two incompatible meanings,
  both paired with interval notation) and §1.2 (`coefficient` bare in era 1, then glossed three
  times in three domains) are the two worth acting on first.
- **5 confirmed R34 replays**, all from one mistake: the boards were checked against the CFU
  instead of the worked example, and three source comments say so in as many words.
- **Two prior claims corrected against the tree**: the d2 replay count is 3, not 6, and
  `GeoCisternPlay` board 0 is not a replay; the `worked_examples` null count is 8, not 13.
- **Shared lesson copy surfaces are clean.** All three candidate findings were rejected on scope.
- **`packages/shared-components` measured**: 126 authored strings, ~76 learner-reachable, and the
  prose in the package itself is clean. The defect the measurement turned up is one level up — a
  development fixture mounted on the learner's main menu.
