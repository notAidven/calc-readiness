Era six's duration ladder, measured on the frame at 1024x580.

The camera is the shipped city camera at the CLOSEST dolly a player has
(VILLAGE_CAMERA.minDistanceRatio 0.5), over era six's own island: grass 46, the
camera 105.8 out at CITY_FOV 32, so one world unit is 19.1 screen pixels here and
9.6 at rest. A barrier stone stands 0.598, which is 11.4 pixels at this dolly and
5.7 at rest; one module of its ladder is 2.0 pixels of side here and about 1.0 of
foreshortened top face.

The four stones stand six units apart on four parallel bands over the same clear
ground at the same heading, which is a COMPARISON PLATE and not a board in play.
Mean weighted luminance over a 7x7 box centred on each stone:

  spent, no course   0.5065
  one course         0.3947
  two courses        0.3205
  a full collar      0.2801

THEY RANK IN ORDER, monotonically, with at least 0.02 between every neighbouring
pair -- which is the doubling doing the job the sheet says it does.

AND THAT IS A PROPERTY OF THE COMPARISON RATHER THAN OF A BOARD. The same four
rungs measured on the board in play -- four bands at four headings over four
slightly different patches of ground -- came back with the middle pair INVERTED by
0.009 and the ends separated by nothing at all. A module is two pixels of side
face and about one of foreshortened top face, which is inside what a background
does to a 7x7 box.

So the honest statement is: SHUT reads anywhere, SET-AGAINST-SPENT reads anywhere,
and FOR HOW LONG reads when a player has two stones to compare and not otherwise.
The count itself is not available at any dolly. The read-out panel is where a
number belongs.
