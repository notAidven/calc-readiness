# Interactive mini-lesson visuals

Lesson visuals are React components in the registry in `apps/web/src/lessons/visuals.tsx`. Because they are ordinary components, a visual can hold state and handle input, which is how a mini-lesson becomes interactive. **Every slide's section 3 visual is interactive and gates teaching:** the advisor NPC "Next" button stays hidden until the learner finishes the interaction. A slide may hold more than one visual (same `after` index), in which case all of them must complete before "Next" advances past section 3.

**Three-section model:** section 1 is the short conceptual lecture (`mini_lesson[i]`). Section 2 is an optional read-only worked example (`worked_examples[i]`, which names its instance in a `prompt` and then walks it in steps). **Section 3 — your widget — is where the learner practices and produces a result.** Do not use the widget as a passive worked walkthrough; section 2 carries the read-only instance. Trim section 1 to a short "Your turn:" hand-off and let the widget ask for production.

Live examples: `PemdasStepSolver` and `OrderMattersPlay` (NUM.2, click-through), `SignedAdditionWalkthrough` (NUM.S, animation that stops before the answer), `DistributeBuilder` (NUM.3, type-and-check), `LikeTermsSorter` and `LikeTermsClassify` (NUM.4, drag-to-bin / locked-miss classify), `PercentSlider` (NUM.5, slider), `CommonDenominatorMultiples` (NUM.F, tappable multiples ladder). Reference diagrams (color keys, mnemonics, rule tables) become light-touch tap-to-reveal / toggle widgets (`PemdasKeyExplore`) or a fill-in-the-blanks version of the diagram itself (`SignRulesTable`, whose 2x2 grid arrives with one cell worked and three to call), not full solve tasks.

## Interaction archetypes

Before writing a new widget, check whether one of the six shared **kinds** in `apps/web/src/lessons/interactive/kinds/` already is the shape. Each is a self-contained component: you pass it the content and the three callbacks, and it brings its own drag/tap handling, wrong-answer copy, reveal and restart button. Read the file's header comment, which states what the kind is for.

| Kind                | Use it when the content is                                                                                                |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `LabelParts`        | naming the parts of one figure or expression (drop a name onto the glyph it belongs to)                                   |
| `MatchPairs`        | a **correspondence** rather than a procedure, and the answers are distinct (each left item equals exactly one right item) |
| `OrderSteps`        | a chain of dependencies, where each step rests on the one before                                                          |
| `SpotTheError`      | a mistake worth recognizing: tap the first wrong line, then choose what belongs there                                     |
| `FillTable`         | "the same move, applied again": worked rows are the pattern, blank rows are what it forces                                |
| `EstimateThenCheck` | a misconception about **magnitude**, where committing a position first is the point                                       |

`LabelParts` has one layout rule worth knowing before you add a statement to it: **a labeling figure is two rows, the statement with its blanks along the top and the terms captioning them underneath, and nothing may be drawn across the line between them.** The blank sits on the line the learner reads — "$\sqrt{49} = 7$ has \_\_\_, \_\_\_, \_\_\_" — and each term is the annotation saying which piece its blank is about, so the row they read IS the row they answer on. A statement's opening math and the connectors between groups (`lead`, `before`) are put on the blanks' row by `.lesson-label__lead`, and the slot precedes the glyph in the DOM as it does on screen, rather than being flipped with `column-reverse`. Do not "fix" a lead's vertical position with `align-self: center`: a part IS blanks-band + gap + terms-band, so the center of one is the boundary between the two rows, and centering there drew `power-parts-label`'s `=` 15.8px into the row of empty boxes. The `[data-raised]` exponent is the one deliberate exception, sitting at the TOP of the terms band so `3^5` still reads as a power. Guards: the band assertions in `widgetDropSlots.browser.test.tsx` and `labelFigureWhole.browser.test.tsx`.

**Which band is content-sized is the whole mechanism, so do not flip it by accident.** The blanks band has a floor (`--label-blank-h`); the terms band is whatever its content needs. That asymmetry is what lets a lead be given exactly the blanks band's height and center its math in it while knowing nothing about the terms: measured over all five figures at four widths, the tallest lead's ink is 30.0px against a 36.0px band. A fixed `height` rather than a `min-height`, so a lead deeper than the band overflows it symmetrically and stays centered on the boxes instead of drifting off them. And **center the math, not the wrapper**: `InlineMath` renders into a span, and a span holding an inline-block is a LINE BOX whose height comes from the font, with the math free to overflow it — centering that wrapper left `x^{\dfrac{5}{4}} \text{ has}` sticking 14.2px out of the top of it. `.lesson-label__lead > span` is a flex container for exactly this reason.

**A shared row is necessary and not sufficient, because a row is a BASELINE and bottom-aligning KaTeX boxes is not the same thing.** A KaTeX box's bottom is its baseline plus the expression's depth, and depth swings with the content: 6.4px under `(0,\, 7)` against 0.0px under a bare `3`. So `align-items: flex-end` over the two puts their baselines 2px apart, and `exp-feature-label`'s `y = 5` sat 2.0px off the terms beside it at three of the four widths measured, for as long as the only assertions were "the terms stay in their own band", which they did. The mechanism that fixes it is `--label-num-line` on `.lesson-label__glyph`: one line box deep enough that no expression overflows it downwards, because a line box's baseline sits a fixed distance above its bottom, decided by the font and the line-height rather than by the content, so aligning the line boxes aligns the math. If you add a deeper expression than the family holds today, that floor is what needs raising. Guarded on baselines, read off KaTeX's own strut by `katexBaseline`, in both files above.

**Derive that family, never list it.** Both guards sweep `visualsBuiltOn("LabelParts")` with a floor under it. It was a written-down three while the tree had grown to five, and the two outside the list — `interval-parts-label` and `exp-feature-label` — inherited every rule above while being asserted by none of them. That is not a hypothetical cost: the 2.0px stagger went unseen because the one figure exhibiting it was one of the two.

Bespoke shapes worth copying when no kind fits:

- **Locked-miss classify** (`PerfectRootClassify`, `LikeTermsClassify`): a call per row, a wrong call locks that row, the gate needs a clean board. This is what makes a two-option question a question.
- **Predict-then-verify** (`RationalizeExplore`): the learner commits to a claim before the evidence is drawn.
- **Multi-slot typed builder** (`DistributeBuilder`, `ExponentLawsBuilder`): several fields checked together.
- **Drag-to-bin** (`LikeTermsSorter`) via `usePickup`.
- **Walkthrough that still asks** (`PowerOfPowerWalkthrough`, `SignedAdditionWalkthrough`): Play/Step lays the structure out and then STOPS, and the learner types the value it was heading for.
- **`foreignObject` buttons over an SVG** (`FractionPartsExplore`, `RatioBarCompare`): the thing being taught is the thing being clicked.
- **`DirectionPad`** for 2-D lattice / coordinate motion.

## Shared mechanics

Every widget hangs off one small layer. `LessonVisual` already wraps each visual in `WidgetFeedbackHost` and already passes `onReopen` and `onPlayingChange`, so a widget only has to opt in.

- **`useWidgetVariants(count, props)`** (`widgetMechanics.ts`) is the mechanism behind every "start over". It returns `{ index, nonce, latch, restart }`. Spend `index` on which parameter set to draw, spend `nonce` as the body's React `key` (remounting clears the body's state, so no hand-written `reset()`), call `latch` when the learner produces the answer and `restart` from the reset button. `restart` deals the NEXT set and calls `onReopen`, which closes the gate again.
- **`WidgetReveal`** (`widgetFeedback.tsx`) is the after-a-wrong-attempt escape: `available={attempted && !solved}`, an `answer` and a `why` (an answer with no reasoning is not allowed), and `onReveal` doing the widget's own fill-in-and-unlock. The shared host decides what a reveal means: before any redraw it teaches and owes the learner a control, and a reveal taken once the instance HAS been redrawn lets them past.
- **`othersRemain` on `WidgetReveal`** is what a multi-answer widget owes. Pass it whenever the instance is asking for answers besides the one being explained — the unnamed slots, the unjoined pairs, the rows still open — and the host offers the learner the instance BACK ("Keep going on this question") as well as a fresh one, with every answer they already got right still standing and the explained one still theirs to produce. It must be a LIVE predicate: on the reveal that explains the last outstanding answer it has to be false, because that answer is the gate and handing the board back there hands the gate over. `revealReAsk.test.ts` refuses a literal `true`, and every kind under `kinds/` passes one. A widget that omits it ends a multi-answer round on one reveal, which is the defect the flag exists to stop.
- **`useRevealReAsk({ reAsk: onRestart, release: onSolved })`** lends the host this instance's redraw and gate. Write it in exactly that form; `revealReAsk.test.ts` matches on the literal binding.
- **`useWidgetSuccess()`** returns a callback that pops the checkmark badge, announces "Correct." and plays the tone. It is REQUIRED, not optional: every judged widget fires it on **each** correct step, so a widget with four judged rows pops four badges, and the last step is not special. Three rules go with it. Never fire it on a reveal, so where a correct branch is shared with the reveal path, gate it (`SignedAdditionWalkthrough`'s `finish(earned)` is the pattern). Fire it on new progress only, never on a re-satisfied condition: attach it to the state transition (the set-add, the `!done` guard, the `next` advance), not the raw predicate, or stepping off a target and back re-pops the badge. And it is independent of `onComplete`, which latches the lesson gate once. It is safe to call outside a host, and delegating to a `kinds/` archetype covers it, since the archetype fires it internally. The only widgets exempt are the ones with no correct-or-wrong moment at all (animation walkthroughs, tap-to-reveal, free exploration); they are pinned by name with a reason in `widgetSuccessCoverage.test.ts`, which sweeps the sources and fails a new widget that fires nothing.
- **`usePickup()`** (`interactive/pickup.ts`) is drag with a tap-then-drop fallback, so a dragging widget stays keyboard- and test-operable.
- **`usePlayingSignal(playing, onPlayingChange)`** tells the lesson player to hold back navigation while a step animation auto-advances.

Anti-brute-force rules, which is most of what separates a question from an activity:

- **Lock a wrong call** rather than letting the learner take the other option. With two options, "try again" is not a question.
- **Neutral option tokens.** Every chip in a choice renders in the same color. Color that marks the correct answer answers the question.
- **Deal options in a derangement.** No option may sit against the slot it belongs to, and the first option must never be the first answer.
- **Commit before evidence.** Do not draw the thing being predicted until the prediction is in.
- **Produce a value.** A widget whose only interaction is uncovering rows that were going to appear anyway gates on nothing.

## The task line comes first (`author` R24)

**`WidgetTask` (`apps/web/src/lessons/widgetTask.tsx`) is the slot, and it is the widget's FIRST child.** The imperative, and what the learner will have produced when it is done, above the controls that do it.

The defect it exists for: the task used to live in `.lesson-interactive__status`, which is the LAST child of every widget because it is the `aria-live` region, **and which the first wrong move overwrites with feedback**. So the task did not merely arrive late in the reading order, after the round counter and the expression and the chips; it left the screen at the first miss and never came back.

- **What belongs in it.** The imperative and the finish condition. NOT the mechanic ("tap a chip, then tap a slot" describes the control, which the control already enforces by refusing a bad tap and saying why), and NOT the rule the task rests on (that is teaching, and it belongs in the prose or on the status line under the task).
- **It must ask for the same KIND of thing the controls offer.** "Tap the even part of 7" beside four chips that are all powers of `p` is a task statement and a control set that disagree about what an answer looks like: the even part of 7 is 6, and 6 was not on offer.
- **`objective` is a required prop on five kinds**: `OrderSteps`, `LabelParts`, `FillTable`, `SpotTheError` and `BinaryRowSort`, so `tsc` refuses a widget built on one of them that forgets it. `MatchPairs` and `EstimateThenCheck` do not carry it. A bespoke widget joins in with one `WidgetTask` line.
- **The guard asserts DOM ORDER, not presence** (`widgetTaskOrder.browser.test.tsx`), because presence is exactly what the overwritten status line already had.
- **Scope, stated honestly:** most widget files still render a status line and no task line. They are bespoke rather than instances of a kind, so there is no contract to extend and no sweep that fixes them at once. Adopt `WidgetTask` in anything you touch; do not read the pinned list as a claim that the codebase is uniform.

## A binary row does not collapse on a miss (`author` R25)

`kinds/BinaryRowSort.tsx` owns this; build on it rather than re-deriving it.

A settled row keeps **both** options on screen, disabled and marked. It used to replace them with a single text verdict, and that threw the teaching away: "$\sqrt{32}$ stays a radical" only instructs while it sits beside "you said it comes out whole". A text verdict TELLS; two options with one marked right and one marked as the learner's miss SHOW. Removing the buttons also made an answered row structurally unlike every unanswered one, so a finished row read as broken rather than as done. The explanation goes in a note under the row it explains, never in the shared status line, which the next attempt overwrites. Guard: `binaryRowMarks.browser.test.tsx`.

## And the question stays put: a call never rewrites the expression

**The thing a row is asking about is the same string before and after it is answered.** This is the same rule as the one above wearing different clothes, and it is worth stating separately because the second costume passed for a feature: `BinaryRowSort` carried an optional `settledLatex`, and the first tap swapped $\sec 90^{\circ}$ for $\sec 90^{\circ} = \dfrac{1}{0}$. Six D9 sorters and four boards in three other domains shipped it before anyone read a settled board as a learner.

- **The learner opened that row to check the call they just made.** Replacing the question with its working is the collapse above applied to the first column instead of to the last two: a miss becomes unreviewable at exactly the moment it is worth reviewing, because what they were asked is no longer on screen beside what they answered.
- **It also moves the board.** The settled reading is the wider string wherever it says anything, and `.lesson-classify` sizes every column across every row at once, so a press on row one drags rows the learner has not touched sideways. A board that shifts under a tap reads as broken however good the new string is.
- **The reading is not lost, it is in the two places already built for it**: the row's `note`, in the wrong valence, under the row, on a miss; and the board's reveal `why`, at greater length, once the board is finished. Put it there. Do not add a second string for the settled state.
- **`AbsCountClassify` (LIN.5's `abs-count-classify`) is the reference**, and always rendered `rule.latex`. Guard: `classifyNumericCallOrder.browser.test.tsx`, over the `visualsBuiltOn("BinaryRowSort")` population plus that reference board, after a correct call and after a wrong one.

**And the same string in the same PLACE, which is a second cause with the same symptom.** Deleting the swap fixes what the row says and leaves where it says it, and a row that goes on stating its expression 7.4px to the left of where it stated it has still moved out from under the learner. Reserve the verdict mark's slot from first paint: render the `.lesson-classify__mark` span always, empty until there is a glyph, and let `.lesson-classify__mark:empty::after` hold it open with the hidden tick.

- **The mechanism is the grid, so the damage is never local.** `--cols-3` sizes its call columns from their content and `.lesson-classify` sizes every column across every row at once, so a glyph that arrives on the first tap re-lays the whole board out. Measured at the 460px cap before the fix: one press on `trg-window-sorter` took every expression 7.4px left and every second call 7.4px right, on rows nobody had touched.
- **Reserve with the real glyph, not with a measured width.** `content: "\2713"` at the mark's own size and `visibility: hidden` makes the slot the width of the thing that will land in it by construction. A hardcoded `min-width` is a number that goes stale the first time the glyph or the font changes.
- **`:empty` is what scopes it.** A widget that renders the mark only once it has a glyph never matches the rule and is unaffected, so adopting this is per-widget and does not reach into anyone else's board.
- **It is a SPATIAL change and it owes looked-at frames**, because it makes every chip permanently as wide as a marked one and the expression track pays for it. Take them at the three widths the numbers resolve differently on: the 920px in-city column, the 460px figure cap, and a 390px phone (a phone viewport, not a narrow box on a desktop). Frame the board a measurement says is widest, not the one that looks it, with rows settled and marks painted. Measured this way on the ten adopters the kind had at the time, and it has twelve since D6, no expression wraps at any of the three; the phone's two-line rows are the stylesheet's own `@container widget (max-width: 380px)` degradation and predate this.
- **Guard both halves in the same test.** The string reading alone is green over a board that never stopped moving, and it looks thorough. `classifyNumericCallOrder.browser.test.tsx` takes a `rowLayout` reading beside it and asserts both after every call, at all three widths.
- **Wait for KaTeX before measuring anything spatial.** `data-latex` is on the element from first render, so a string comparison is happy long before the math has a box. The first layout reading here caught its own harness: every expression measuring `left === right === 339.0` before the press and a real box after it, which is a picture of the typesetter arriving and not of anything a learner did.

## A column of plain numbers is right-aligned, and the kind works that out from its own rows

**A first column whose every row is a bare measure is aligned from the RIGHT, because a number column is read down its right edge.** Reported on TRG.1: aligned from the start of the track, $-320^{\circ}$ put its minus sign where $400^{\circ}$ puts a digit, so the units of one measure sat under the tens of the next and the four degree signs ended in four different places. Measured at the 920px in-city column, that ragged right edge was 13.1px on `trg-coterminal-sorter` and 21.5px on `trg-window-sorter`.

- **Derive the population from the ROWS, not from a prop and not from a list of board ids.** What makes a column a number column is that everything in it is a number, which is a fact about the rows the kind was handed and is visible inside the kind. `BinaryRowSort` asks `rows.every(isPlainMeasure)` and sets `.lesson-classify--numeric` on the board, so the thirteenth adopter whose rows are measures is aligned without anybody remembering to ask, and an adopter that changes its rows to expressions stops being aligned as a number column in the same edit that stops it being one. A prop would put the decision on twelve adopters one at a time.
- **`every`, not `some`.** One $\sec 90^{\circ}$ among four measures is a column of mixed strings, and right-aligning those aligns the ends of unrelated things. The predicate is deliberately strict for the same reason: `w = 5.02` is a reading about a number and `8 \le w \le 21` is a comparison between two, so neither is one.
- **Fill the track and align the CONTENTS. Never re-pin the cell to `justify-self: end`.** That is what the existing `start` exists to refuse: pinned to `end`, a cell wider than its track grew leftwards off the card, which is how a comparison lost its left-hand operand. `justify-self: stretch` with `text-align: right` puts the digits at the right of a box that can never be narrower than they are, because `min-content` on the column is the expression's own width, so an over-wide cell has nowhere to slide to.
- **A number column is not a column on a phone, so exclude it there and assert the exclusion.** Under `@container widget (max-width: 380px)` the expression spans the whole row on a line of its own; right-aligned it floats off to the card's right edge with its two calls underneath on the left. `text-align: start` inside the container query, and the guard measures the phone column too, so a container rule that quietly stopped applying fails rather than leaving the wide-width claim excusing a layout nobody chose.
- **Guard the derivation in BOTH directions, with a floor on each side** (`classifyNumericCallOrder.browser.test.tsx`). The boards measured right-aligned are asserted as a SET equal to the two whose rows are plain measures, so a predicate that has widened fails by name and so does one that has stopped matching; the boards measured left-aligned carry a floor, so a predicate matching everything cannot pass by leaving nothing on the other side. Read "right-aligned" as two facts: the right edges agree AND the left edges do not, since a board whose rows happen to paint the same width satisfies both alignments at once and would meet a one-sided claim while being aligned neither way. Both halves were watched fail: forced to match everything, the set equality names the eight boards that wrongly joined and the floor reports "only 2 boards still align from the start"; forced to match nothing, the set equality comes back empty.

## The chip label is already centred vertically. Do not "fix" it

**A report that a button's text sits off centre is worth measuring before it is worth changing, and this one did not survive the measurement.** Every chip on all twelve boards on the kind holds its content centred to 0.0px, at the 920px in-city column, the 460px figure cap and the 330.8px phone column, unanswered and with every row answered. Nothing was changed; the reading is pinned in `classifyNumericCallOrder.browser.test.tsx` instead, so the finding can fail if it stops being true.

- **Measure the CONTENT, not the label.** The chip is `flex-wrap: wrap` holding the label beside `.lesson-classify__mark`, and on a narrow card the mark takes a flex line of its own: `perfect-root-classify` at the phone column then has 18.4px more room below the label than above it, and every one of those pixels is the tick sitting in it. Read that way a correct layout looks 18px out, which is how it gets "fixed".
- **Do not centre glyph bounds.** The letters are not symmetric in their line box and are not supposed to be: "Keep it" has a descender and sits 9.5px/4.5px, "even" is all x-height at 13.5px/8.5px, "Coterminal" has neither at 9.5px/8.5px. Centring ink instead of line boxes puts the two calls on one row on two different baselines, which is the defect and not the fix.
- **Floor the wrapped state, or the claim is green over the only case that can fail it.** One flex line is centred by `align-items` alone, so a sweep in which nothing wraps asserts nothing about `align-content`. Injecting `flex-wrap: nowrap` reports 0.0px over 600 chips with 0 of them wrapped — a perfect pass measuring the wrong thing — and the floor is what turns that into a failure.

## The correctness palette is reserved (`author` R26)

**`CIVIC` green and `BRICK` red are the correctness channel and they report on the LEARNER'S ACTION.** Green means their pick was right, red means it was not. That is the whole of what they may say, and `svgKit.tsx`'s token comments are the source of truth.

- **A standing mark that is not a verdict takes `SIGNAL_DEEP` amber.** The right answer on a row the learner MISSED is not a verdict on anybody, because they did not pick it; green there would credit a pick that was never made. Same for the line a two-stage widget is mid-repair on.
- **A non-colour carrier is always required.** Every state also names itself in its accessible name and in a glyph, and the learner's own pick is solid where a standing mark is dashed. Fill is reserved for a correct pick, so a fill never means "wrong". No state is colour alone, ever.
- **The subject of the question stays ink.** Tinting the classified expression by the learner's correctness puts a third meaning on the same palette and makes a perfectly good $\sqrt{32}$ look like the thing that was wrong.
- **Binding a label to a slot uses weight, style or position, never hue.** A widget that colours matched pairs to show which goes with which has spent the correctness channel on bookkeeping, and a correct pick then reads as a wrong one. This is the strict form of the neutral-option-tokens rule above.

Guards: `spotTheErrorPalette.browser.test.tsx` and `binaryRowMarks.browser.test.tsx`.

One typographic rule: **never set a `font-family` on a widget surface**, in CSS or in a `style` prop. KaTeX owns the face of all math, so the declaration cannot reach the math at all; the only thing it reaches is the plain words sharing the container, and those are prose that belongs in the body font. The sole exception is `.lesson-interactive__input`, which keeps a monospace stack so typed digits hold their column.

## A layout every instance owes belongs to the container, not to an opt-in

If every child of a container needs the same treatment, the container is where it goes. Written as an opt-in — a prop, or a selector naming one caller — it fixes the instance in front of you and leaves the identical defect in every sibling, where it is now harder to find because a rule for it appears to exist.

Two worked examples, both real:

- **A CSS sibling selector is the same mistake with no prop to notice.** `.lesson-player__row` stretches its children, so the Check button grew to match whatever answer widget sat beside it. The fix shipped as `.cr-math-answer ~ button { align-self: flex-start }` — correct, and scoped to the single caller it was written for, so `expr_builder`, `number_line` and `.cr-rational-answer` kept their full-height green slabs. It is now `align-items: flex-start` on the row itself. When you find yourself writing a selector that names one caller to fix a layout, ask what the OTHER children of that container do about it.
- **A class name with no rules behind it is the same thing at zero.** `cr-expr-builder-input`, `cr-number-line-input` and `cr-graph-input` matched nothing in `styles.css`, and `packages/shared-components` ships no CSS at all, so what a learner met was drawn by the user agent: the browser's own `<fieldset>` frame, fields jammed edge to edge, inputs stretched by a rule written for one bare `<input>`. Grep the sheet for a widget's class before assuming its appearance is designed. Guard: `answerRowChrome.browser.test.tsx`.

## Where things live

- **Shared SVG primitives**: `apps/web/src/lessons/svgKit.tsx` (KaTeX renderers `SvgMath`, `EquationRow`, `InlineMath`; color tokens `LABEL/SOFT/LINE/CIVIC/BRICK/SIGNAL`, `PEMDAS`, `PemdasRole`, `EqToken`, `stackGapMid`; and for anything drawn on a coordinate plane, `plotLabelFrame`, `labelSpot`, `labelHalfW`, `labelHalfH`, `PlotLabel`, `interiorTicks`, `fittingTicks`). Import these here to keep widgets cycle-free (do NOT import primitives from `visuals.tsx`). There is deliberately no font token: see the rule below.
- **One widget per file**: `apps/web/src/lessons/interactive/<Name>.tsx`.
- **Registry + id union**: `apps/web/src/lessons/visuals.tsx` (`LessonVisualId`, `REGISTRY`). Import the widget here and add its entry.
- **Gating flag**: `gates?: boolean` on `LessonVisualPlacement` in `apps/web/src/lessons/types.ts`.
- **Gating plumbing**: `AdvisorLesson.tsx` threads `onComplete` into `LessonVisual` for every gating visual and withholds `onNext` until `doneVisuals` (a set of completed visual ids) records all gating visuals on the current paragraph. Already generic, no per-lesson edits needed.
- **Styles**: shared `.lesson-interactive*` classes in `apps/web/src/styles.css` (status `[data-state]`, controls, chips, steps, range, reset, **direction pad**) plus any widget-specific classes.
- **Shared direction pad**: `apps/web/src/lessons/interactive/DirectionPad.tsx` — keyboard-layout arrow keys (`↑` on top, `← ↓ →` on the bottom row). Use it for lattice / coordinate steppers instead of a row of "Left" / "Right" / "Up" / "Down" text buttons.

## Component contract

Export `function <Name>(props: LessonWidgetProps)` (from `widgetMechanics.ts`: `onComplete`, `onReopen`, `onPlayingChange`). A widget built on one of the shared kinds is a thin wrapper that supplies content and forwards `variant.latch` / `variant.restart`; a bespoke widget splits into a wrapper holding `useWidgetVariants` and a body keyed on `variant.nonce`. It must:

1. Manage its own state and render a `<div className="lesson-interactive lesson-interactive--<kind>">` containing the SVG and/or native controls.
2. Open the gate **exactly once**, when the learner reaches the correct final state. Use `variant.latch`, which is idempotent, rather than calling `onComplete` by hand.
3. Offer a retry when solved (a `.lesson-interactive__reset` button). A restart must deal a **different** instance and re-close the gate, which is what `variant.restart` does; dealing the same numbers back asks the learner to retype an answer they already know, and leaving the gate latched lets them walk past without answering. Name the button for what it deals ("Start over with a different sum"), not "Start over".
4. Show an `aria-live` status line (`.lesson-interactive__status` with `data-state="prompt" | "wrong" | "solved"`).
5. Stay usable without a mouse: use native `<input type="range">` (slider) and real `<button>` elements; for pointer-drag widgets add a click/step fallback (buttons) so it is keyboard-operable and testable. **Do not use `<select>` dropdowns** (they read as multiple choice, not interaction). For 2-D lattice / marker motion, use `DirectionPad` (keyboard layout), not a horizontal row of word labels.
6. For step-by-step animations, drive the frame counter with a `setTimeout`/`setInterval` that is cleaned up in a `useEffect` return (no timer leaks), and expose a "Play" (auto) plus a "Step" (advance one, keyboard/test fallback) and a "Replay". Fire `onComplete` once when the final state is reached, whether via Play or Step.
7. Never leak lint: avoid `role`/`aria-*` on plain `<div>`s (use real `<button>` elements); give list items stable keys.
8. **Typeset every math string with KaTeX** (see the `lesson-katex` and `svg` skills): `SvgMath` / `EquationRow` inside the SVG, `InlineMath` in the buttons, chips, field labels and status line. `LessonProse` is wrong in a control because it emits a block `<p>`. A status line that mixes prose and math is a `ReactNode`, not a string: `<>Try again: <InlineMath latex="-7 + 4" /> is not 3.</>`. Because `InlineMath` makes `textContent` unreliable (KaTeX's MathML duplicates every glyph), give each math control an `aria-label` spelling the value out in words, and have tests match on that or on `hasMath` from `testHarness.ts`.
9. **A typed blank goes IN the equation, not in a row of boxes under it.** When the widget asks the learner to fill in a formula, scaffold the formula on screen and make each blank the input, in the position of the value it holds: `m = \dfrac{[ ] - [ ]}{[ ] - [ ]}`. Build it in HTML with the `.lesson-equation` classes (`__row`, `__stage`, `__body`, `__frac`, `__part`, `__bar`, `__slot`, `__slot--done`, `__slot--locked`) and KaTeX for the fixed parts, because an `<input>` cannot live inside KaTeX rendered into a `foreignObject`; the diagram above it stays SVG. `LinearSlopeWalkthrough` is the worked example: three stages, one open at a time, a locked stage showing dashed slots with nothing focusable, and a settled slot keeping its outline so a negative still reads apart from the minus in front of it.

10. **Coordinate planes follow the plane rules in the `svg` skill**: tag the plot rect `data-plot-frame`, place every on-plane label with `labelSpot` + `PlotLabel` inside a `plotLabelFrame`, and number only the interior ticks.

11. **Typeset math cannot be case-mangled any more, so the only remaining way to ship a wrong-case variable is to not typeset it.** `text-transform` inherits and KaTeX renders math as ordinary text nodes, so for a long time any container that upper-cased its own label upper-cased the variables inside it: `w = \dfrac{P - 2l}{2}` shipped as `W = \dfrac{P - 2L}{2}`. That is closed at the root now, by `text-transform: none` on the global `.katex` rule in `styles.css`, and there is nothing per-widget left to remember.

    Read the history, because the shape of the mistake is more useful than the rule. It was first found on `.lesson-interactive__slider-label`, and the fix shipped as an opt-in modifier (`--mixed`) plus a line in this skill scoped to _slider and select labels_. Scoping a rule by where the defect was first seen rather than by what makes it possible is what the QA rule in `.cursor/rules/qa-verification.mdc` warns about, and it cost five more boards across three classes: `.lesson-equation__stage` upper-cased the LIN.3 and LIN.4 equation column and D4's stone bins, because nobody had filed it as a label, while `percent-slider` and `common-denominator-multiples` simply never took the opt-out that had been written for exactly their two classes. Any container is a candidate. Only the math was ever the invariant.

    What you still owe:

    - **Put a variable in `InlineMath`, never in a string.** `{rule.target} =` is a plain text node, has no `.katex` for the global rule to attach to, and is upper-cased exactly as before. ``<InlineMath latex={`${rule.target} =`} />`` is both the fix and the correct typography for a variable.
    - **A math-bearing `.lesson-equation__stage` also takes `lesson-equation__stage--math`.** The base class is small-caps label typography: its `letter-spacing` opens gaps between KaTeX's atom spans, and its size sets the stage a third smaller than the same expression beside it. The modifier drops the spacing and raises the size to what the fixed 4.6rem track can hold. Leave the base class's `text-transform` in place; `.katex` refuses it, and the stage still carrying it is what keeps the guard measuring a real container.
    - **Leave the `--mixed` modifiers alone.** They are now doing one job, which is keeping the words beside the math out of small caps where that was the intent, and they no longer have anything to do with case-mangling math.

    Guard: `mathCaseTransform.browser.test.tsx`. Its population is derived, every visual id any lesson pack places, and it makes three claims: every `.katex` computes `text-transform: none`; no equation stage carries math as its own text, which is the half the global rule structurally cannot see; and a math-bearing stage's expression stays inside its own column. It does not reach a stage a learner has to unlock first, `stone-pieces-sorter` being the one, so that board's stage is covered by the rule and not by an assertion.

Wiring after the component exists:

- Add the id to `LessonVisualId` and a `REGISTRY[id]` entry `{ Svg: <Name>, caption }`. The caption is Markdown rendered through `LessonProse`, so its math is KaTeX too: `"Same-tier operations go left to right: apply $\\div$ before $\\times$."`.
- In the lesson JSON, point the placement at the new id and set `"gates": true`: `{ "after": i, "id": "<id>", "gates": true }`.

## Authoring rules

- **Every visual is interactive and gated.** Every `visuals` placement carries `"gates": true`. There are no static-only visuals in a finished lesson. When a paragraph holds two widgets, both must complete before "Next" appears.
- **Reference diagrams get light-touch interactions.** Color keys, mnemonics, and rule tables become tap-to-reveal / toggle widgets with a clear completion state, not contrived solve tasks. Worked examples become click-through / build / animated interactions.
- **Reduce text, and do NOT hand off with a "Your turn: ...".** Trim every paired paragraph to a short lead-in; the paragraph ends when the teaching ends. `author` R9 reversed this skill's earlier advice to close each paragraph with a "Your turn: ..." line, because the task statement belongs in the widget (R24 says where in the widget), and the validator now warns on one in prose. The shipped D1 and D5 packs are full of them and predate the rule; do not copy them. No em dashes (commas / colons / periods only), same rule as all learner-facing copy.
- **Widget copy uses the words that already exist.** Say "drag and drop", the phrase every learner already knows, rather than coining a description of the gesture; `usePickup` gives you a tap-then-drop fallback, so the copy has to cover both without inventing a name for either. Coined vocabulary is banned outright in learner-facing text (`author` R23: "crook" was the one that got us), and placeholder copy that shipped is the same defect wearing a deadline.
- **A label slot holds a TERM, not a gloss** (`author` R21). A name being dropped onto a glyph is the vocabulary the lesson is teaching, so "what the symbol hands back" teaches a paraphrase where **result** was the word. A multi-word phrase built around a verb is the tell.
- **Sibling option labels all answer the question the prompt asks** (`author` R22). Two bins named by their radicand and a third named by what the radicand does are three accurate labels answering two different questions. Nothing local catches this; state the prompt's question and read every label against it.
- **Different instance from the CFU.** The widget's example should not reuse the CFU's exact numbers.
- **No dropdowns.** Do not use `<select>`. Vary the widget to the concept: number-line hops/animation for signed addition, area model taps for distribution, drag-to-bin or tap-classify for like terms, a slider for percent-of, a tappable multiples ladder for a common denominator, click-through tiers for order of operations, a `DirectionPad` for rise/run or coordinate markers.
- **Theme tokens only.** Colors come from `svgKit` tokens / CSS custom properties so widgets match the palette in light and dark contexts. The widget also renders on the combined review page (ungated), so it must read correctly with no `onComplete`. Per-token color stays a CSS `color`, never `\textcolor` — KaTeX cannot parse `var(--civic)`.
- **All math is KaTeX; no Unicode arithmetic.** No `×`, `÷`, `−`, `2/3` or `25%` in a widget string, in the SVG or in a control. Write LaTeX: `\times`, `\div`, `-`, `\dfrac{2}{3}` (`\tfrac` in a tight control), `25\%`. Arrow glyphs on `DirectionPad` keys are UI chrome, not math operators.
