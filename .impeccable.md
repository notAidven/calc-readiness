## Design Context

### Users

High-school and college students checking and rebuilding the prerequisite math they need for
calculus. They may arrive uncertain or frustrated, often on a school laptop or phone, and need to
understand the next useful action without learning the product's internal vocabulary.

### Brand Personality

Grounded, encouraging, and quietly playful. The experience should build confidence without talking
down to learners, and should feel dependable enough that mistakes—by the learner or the product—are
safe to acknowledge and recover from.

### Aesthetic Direction

A tactile, inhabited learning world: parchment, stone, brass, land, and constructed objects rather
than generic SaaS chrome. The interface is light, readable, and direct. Decoration should reinforce
place and progress; it must never obscure navigation, feedback, or math.

### Design Principles

1. Make the next action unmistakable, with concise student-facing language.
2. Preserve the learning world's material character instead of importing generic support widgets.
3. Treat errors as recoverable events and always explain what the learner can do next.
4. Keep every core action keyboard reachable, screen-reader named, and at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels.
5. Adapt the full experience to phones; do not hide critical functions at narrow widths.

### Public metrics extension

The metrics page also serves the beta owner and people deciding whether to join or support the
beta. It should read as a concise editorial progress ledger, not an admin console: lead with how
many learners joined, show the signup-to-completion sequence, label what every number counts, and
publish aggregates only—never names, emails, answers, or individual timelines.
