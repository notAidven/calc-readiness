## The propagation job: **tipping**

D-CRISIS-8 ruled the four propagation crises apart on deliberate axes so a player never meets the
same mental model twice, and `crisis-reference-packs/README.md:176-181` states that separation as a
fact about the **picture** rather than about the mechanics:

| Era | What propagates | Along what | What the player watches | The read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Hard Winter | Frost | Tile adjacency | An edge, and which way it is moving | **Creeping** |
| **4 Succession** | **Allegiance** | **Nothing spatial at all** | **An object arriving at a plinth before the decision lands** | **Tipping** |
| 6 Plague | Infection | Authored links between buildings | A line in the floor, and what has travelled along it | **Reaching** |
| 9 Intrusion | Compromise | Nothing visible | A table of readings, and how old each one is | **Suspecting** |

**Four different visual jobs**: watch an edge, watch an object appear, watch a line, read a board.
`README.md:183-186` says nothing in this pass may blur them, so **a shortening pass that made the
three crisis sheets rhyme would destroy exactly what that ruling protects.** Era four's row, argued
in era four's own terms:

- **Against the frost.** *"The frost's question is 'how far has it got'; era four's is 'which one is
  next'."* Era four has **no edge and nothing to watch move**: the player watches an **object
  appear**, at a place, once per cycle (`2026-08-01-succession-crisis-art-brief.md:236-240`).
- **Against era six.** *"Era six's board has lines on it and era four's has none."* **Era four draws
  no connections at all.** Its boundary stones **enclose**; they never **join** (`:242-245`).
- **Against era nine.** *"Era nine's board is mostly unknown and era four's is fully known and
  getting worse."* **Era four hides nothing**; the pressure is the cost of acting (`:247-251`).
- **One sentence for a review:** *era four is the crisis where the player can see everything and is
  losing anyway* (`:253-254`).

**Frames 2, 11 and 5 carry it, in that order — absence, waiting, landing.** A bare pedestal is not
drifting this cycle and that absence is as informative as the block; a block waiting at the foot
under slack shear-legs says somebody is about to raise a statue here, and **whose**; the block
hoisted is the same object, moved. D-CRISIS-16 charges for hesitation and a charge nobody saw coming
is a punishment rather than a stake, so **render 2, 11 and 5 first and judge them before spending
anything on the rest** (`:227-230`).

## The shared direction

*Read once. Every brief below inherits all of it, and none of them restates it.*

- **One object, isolated on plain parchment `#ECE4D3` and contained within its own footprint.**
  Isometric low-poly, one fixed camera tilted about 49 degrees down, no horizon tilt, framed 1400 by
  900. **The parchment is a background rather than a surface**: the object stands on it, nothing is
  drawn on it, and nothing stands beyond it. There is no ground, no horizon and no sky. Parchment is
  the Art Bible's own ground tone (`docs/Game_Visual_Design_Brief.md:157`), so an object sits on it
  the way it will sit on the island.
- **Classical port city, four materials.** Dressed ashlar limestone in lime mortar as the body
  `#D2C8AA`, flat-faced, **its thin level coursed joints the material itself, so a wall reads as
  assembled from equal blocks rather than piled from found ones**; terracotta pan-and-cover roof tile
  `#B4674A` with an ordered ridge and antefixes at the eaves, the era's one strong colour and its
  only roof; lime stucco just off the body hue where ashlar is not affordable, a surface with nothing
  to hide; triangulated timber roof truss, its end visible in a pediment. **Marble sits inside the
  stone as an accent only** — a threshold, a column drum, a counting slab — never as a whole body.
  **Marble is `#CCC8BD`, and it separates from the limestone on cut and on colour both.** On cut: a
  marble accent is **one monolithic piece with no coursing joint anywhere in it**, where limestone is
  always assembled from equal blocks under thin level joints. On colour: `#CCC8BD` is **the limestone's
  own lightness and its own hue angle with the warmth taken out** — L\* 80.6 against 80.7, hue 95°
  against 95°, chroma **6.0 against 16.3** — so marble is that same pale stone drained of its yellow
  rather than a whiter stone. **Holding the value is the point rather than a compromise**: a counting
  slab lies flat and shows the camera its top face, and a paler marble would take that face into the
  parchment behind it. Neither channel is sufficient alone and the cut is not retired by the colour.
  Derived at owed item 9, which states plainly that this hue was proposed here and not looked up.
  **The repeated bay is the ornament**: colonnades and counted modules at constant spacing, and
  nothing decorative that is not also a way of building.
- **Three tone steps per surface under one upper-left key** — top face lightened about 26 percent,
  the hue on the left, the right darkened about 28. **No runtime lights**: no cast shadow, no ambient
  occlusion, no specular highlight, no rim light, no gradient. Never describe an object by light
  falling on it.
- **The subject fills the frame**, centred, its silhouette at least two thirds of the frame's height,
  nothing clipped. **Height is measured on the built object only**: a hoist, a plume, a ghost or
  anything else above it does not count toward it. *(That clause is
  #411's and is the one part of it the
  parchment does not retire — era one's pass three returned a hearth about 100 pixels tall under a
  smoke column carrying the total to 682, satisfying the number and missing the point.)* **Where a
  frame holds two objects, the two thirds is carried by the one the frame's mark sits on**, never by
  the larger thing it stands in or on. On frames 7 to 10 that is the boundary stone and not the paving
  panel it is set into, which the first run got the wrong way round — see "When the subject is a piece
  of ground".
- **One state per frame**, so the sheet stays an asset set rather than a diagram. Every comparison is
  composited afterwards from the single renders, never asked for in a prompt.
- **Every side must read**, because the camera orbits a full 360°, and **the frame carries no label**
  — no caption, legend, annotation, leader line, arrow, numeral or text. The reading key is this
  document.

**Absent on purpose, because each is a later era's signature move:** the pointed arch, the buttress,
the timber frame expressed outside a wall, the dome raised on a drum, window glass at any size, and
structural iron.

### The seven rulings a picture can break

**The rule that cut 67 fed clauses to seven: a prohibition survives only if breaking it produces a
picture that is wrong about the DESIGN rather than one that is merely ugly.** Everything the
parchment retired went with the ground it was about — the floating slab, the detached tile, the void
behind the floor, the fill or tint over the paving — because there is no ground left in the frame to
get wrong. The generic ones went on era one's measured ground that its failed run held essentially
all of them without being asked. **What replaced the rest is a change of grammar**: era two's briefs
carry **zero** *"no X"* clauses across all ten briefs, measured, and separate a confusable pair by naming
the axis instead, which is what every *"must not resemble"* below does. **Feed these seven verbatim,
last, and add nothing.** Each carries its warrant, because the warrant is what makes it un-droppable.

> - **Nothing joins one thing to another.** No line, band, path, route, edge, link or run of anything
>   that **connects** two districts, buildings or plinths. Era four's boundary stones **enclose** and
>   never **join**: a line of separate stones turning a corner round an area is right, and anything
>   running from one building, plinth or district to another is wrong.
> - **No state is carried by hue.** Allegiance is carried by pose and by what stands on a pedestal,
>   never by a tint, a fill or a wash.
> - **Nothing in the frame reads as elapsed time.** A cycle is shown by an object having arrived, and
>   by nothing else.
> - **Every mark is cut into stone**, a groove with a shadow line lying inside it, never a line drawn
>   or painted on top.
> - **The two cut marks are a chevron and a ring.** Both are shapes, and nothing on any stone is
>   readable as a character.
> - **Everything is intact.** The city has stopped working, not fallen.
> - **No mastery gold `#FFB703`**, and no gilding.

**Why each survived, for the reviewer rather than the generator.** **No joining** is the propagation
separation stated as a prohibition and is the one a generator is likeliest to break, since a line is
what it reaches for the moment it is told something spreads (`:242-245`). **No hue** protects what the
plinth frames exist to prove: a tint would settle the question the whole sheet is asking. **No elapsed
time** is INV-CRISIS-1, which forbids a cycle that appears to turn on its own (`:219-222`), and a dial
or a countdown in a still frame makes the same false promise. **Cut, not drawn** is the era's whole
material logic against the generative default of drawing a line. **Two marks, both shapes** is the pack
convention that no plate carries a word (`README.md:206-209`), and classical stone is the likeliest
surface in the run for an inscription to appear uninvited. **Intact** is the costliest confusion here,
because *shuttered* and *sacked* are one short step apart. **Mastery gold** is reserved outright across
every era.

**The word *connects* in the first ruling, and the positive case after it, are 2026-08-02's — and they
close a contradiction rather than loosen a rule.** Fed verbatim to frame 1, whose own subject is a run
of boundary stones among three pedestals and three buildings, the earlier form forbade that frame's own
marks, and *"feed these seven verbatim, last, and add nothing"* leaves a generator nothing to resolve
it with. **The first run resolved it correctly anyway**: `wide-master.png` came back with the stones as
separate cubes turning a corner round an area, nothing filling it and no territory read. So this is a
hazard closed rather than a failure repaired, and the change is the smallest one that stops the fed
sentence contradicting the single frame it lands hardest on.

### When the subject is a piece of ground

**Frames 7 to 10 have a subject that is ground itself**: a boundary stone is *set into the paving
along one of its own joint lines*, and the joint line is half the subject. *"No ground in the
picture"* and *"the subject is a piece of ground"* need reconciling, and getting it wrong is how the
floating slab came back four times.

**A piece of ground is drawn as a made object, never as a piece of terrain.** A paved panel is a
manufactured thing — cut units, fitted joints, a dressed outer edge — and it goes on the parchment as
any other object does: complete, centred, within its own footprint.

**The rule that blocks a frame is that the panel is BOUNDED, and 2026-08-02 promoted it to that
position on the evidence of the nineteen returned frames.** It used to sit behind *"only a mark that
is IN the floor gets a panel"*, which is a rule about **whether** a panel may appear. That turned out
to be the wrong rule to lead with, and the run says so in a way no argument could:

- **Fourteen of the nineteen frames are sent straight onto the parchment with no paving at all, and
  fourteen of fourteen came back standing on a paving panel.** Frames 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 11, 12, 13, 14,
  15, 16, 17, 18 and 19, checked by looking at each returned image. The clause was obeyed **zero
  times**, so it is not a clause a generator will honour.
- **And in eleven of those fourteen it cost nothing whatever**, because the panel came back
  *bounded*: a made patch of paving lying on the parchment with the parchment plainly visible round
  it, which is what this section asks a panel to be in the first place. Frames 3, 4, 5, 11, 12, 13,
  14, 15, 17, 18 and 19.
- **The paving ran to all four frame edges in four frames** — 2, 6 and 16, which are sent to the
  parchment, and 10, which is entitled to a panel. **In those the frame stops being a picture of an
  object and becomes a view of a floor**, which is the failure the whole rewrite exists to prevent.
- **The one frame that lost its subject is the one entitled to a panel whose panel went unbounded.**
  In `boundary-stone-both.png` the paving fills the frame and the boundary stone is gone — returned
  as a marked flagstone lying flush in the paving, with no block at all. **So the defect tracks
  unboundedness and not the panel's presence**, measured over nineteen frames rather than argued.

> **The parchment is visible on all four sides of everything in the frame, and no paving ever touches
> a frame edge.** Whatever is in shot — object, panel, or an object on a panel — sits complete inside
> the frame with plain parchment round it.

**So a bounded panel under something that merely stands on the floor is tolerated rather than
requested.** It is not a defect and it is not worth a regeneration. **But it is never described here
and never conditioned on**, because a panel the brief did not ask for is scenery the generator added,
and the moment a brief starts describing it, it becomes a second subject. **Only a mark that is IN the
floor gets a panel that is part of the SUBJECT** — described, conditioned on its anchor, and
measured — because there the joint line is half the reading. That is what the IN-the-floor
distinction still decides, and it is a narrower job than the one it used to be given.

**The panel and the slab separate on the edge, and that is the whole of it.** A made panel ends where
a paving unit ends: its outer edge is a **dressed square arris in the same stone as the top face, one
course thick**, finished the way a kerb stone is, and the panel lies flat on the parchment with
nothing beneath it. A floating slab ends in **a section through the ground**: a thick body with an
underside, a torn or tapered edge, soil or rock below the paving, and parchment visible under its far
side. The first is masonry, the second is landscape lifted out — and the second is what came back
four times on era one.

**A third edge came back that neither of those describes, and it is the parchment's own doing.** In
frames 7, 8 and 9 the panel ends in **a torn, deckled sheet edge** — paving on a ragged rectangle of
textured paper, so the parchment stopped being a background and became a *sheet* lying in the picture.
**A panel's edge is masonry**: dressed, square, one course thick, and never torn, deckled, ragged,
feathered or vignetted. Parchment is what the object stands on and is never a thing the object stands
*at the edge of*.

**So describe a panel by its material, never by its size**, and **let its extent follow from the
stone set into it** — the same construction era six's and era nine's sheets use for a band and for a
duct marker. *"Only a few cells of ground at most"* was tried on era one and read as permission to
draw ground with a visible edge, which is the slab; that phrasing is deleted and must not come back.

**The stone carries the two thirds and the panel does not.** The height floor and this section used
to point at two different objects with no rule for choosing, and **the first run of frames 7 to 10
spent the floor on the panel every time**: in `boundary-stone-unmarked.png` the paving panel measures
about **90 percent** of frame height and the boundary stone itself about **37**, and by
`boundary-stone-both.png` the stone had lost its height altogether and come back as a marked flagstone
flush in the paving. That is `#411`'s hearth-under-the-smoke-column exactly — a floor satisfied by the
wrong object — and it costs the thing these four frames are for, since the mark has to be *"legible on
a small top face"*. **So the two thirds is measured on the stone: its block and top face, at least two
thirds of the frame's height, and the panel is never the tallest thing in the frame.** The
floating-slab risk does not come back with it, because the panel's edge is still a dressed square
arris one course thick lying flat on the parchment, and because that edge was never a question of how
wide the panel is.

### One proposal grammar, and it is the see-through stone

**Frames 6 and 15 are this sheet's two proposals and they used to be drawn two different ways.**
Frame 6 asked for the stone object rendered thinner; frame 15 asked for *"a raised outline of the
panel's shape only… with no cut chevron and no filled face"*. That second grammar cannot satisfy
`planned-against-committed.png`, whose claim is that **a proposal and a fact are the same shape
differing only in translucency and value** — an outline differs from a filled panel in **fill**. It
also sat one dash-pattern from the *"must not resemble a UI overlay"* prohibition both frames inherit,
whose apparatus list opens with *"a dashed or dotted outline"*.

**The first run settles which grammar to keep, and it is not the outline.** In
`civic-building-inscription-proposed.png` the outline came back as a **solid dark raised moulding**
with its own shading — committed masonry ornament, carrying no translucency at all for the composited
sheet's claim to rest on — and in a pointed lozenge cartouche rather than the panel's own rectangular
slab and bracket, so it was not the panel's silhouette either. Frame 6's grammar came back
see-through, with the paving joints faintly legible through the figure's body.

> **A proposal on this sheet is the stone object itself, whole, rendered see-through at a little over
> half opacity in the dark end of the stone family, standing in its exact committed place.** Never an
> outline, never hollow, never a line drawn round an empty middle.

**And a proposal keeps its cut mark**, which is the part likeliest to be dropped as an over-literal
reading of *not yet cut*. Frame 11 already argues it for the drift block: a mark that says only that
*a block is there*, rather than **whose** block it is, turns a warning into a surprise. A proposed
inscription panel with its chevron removed says *a panel is coming* in the one crisis whose whole
question is *which one is next* — and the chevron is part of the shape the composited sheet claims is
unchanged.

### Read the plates for shape only

**The plates draw the failure this rewrite undoes, and that is measured rather than asserted.** Era
one's pack gives its seven detail plates no horizon at all and their subjects occupy **18.2 to 25.0
percent** of frame; the three new packs give every plate a horizon, spending **68 to 72 percent** of
frame height on sky and water, and their detail subjects occupy **3.9 to 15.6 percent** — no overlap
between the two sets. Succession's own `03-district-boundary-on-paving.png` measures **5.5 percent**,
inside the band era one's pass two came back at. Method and command in era one's retired sheet.

**So every citation below reads *shape from*, and it means the object's silhouette and proportions
and nothing else — never the camera, the ground, the sky, the framing or the side-by-side
arrangement.**

## How the comparison sheets get assembled

regeneration, no relighting. Every render must come back 1400 by 900 at the same camera and scale,
which is what makes a straight tile legal, and the first run came back at three different sizes
instead. **Tile, do not scale**: each panel keeps its own frame at full size, so the sheet is
1400*n* by 900. The panels butt, with no gutter, rule, border or caption, and the reading key is this
document (`README.md:206-209`).

### All six sheets need one object twice, so all six need an anchor

**Every *"the pedestal of frame 2"*, *"the stone and panel of frame 7"*, *"the building of frame 12"*
and *"the slab of frame 16"* is an identity requirement across two independent generations, and two
text prompts do not return the same object.** This used to be recorded as the proposal sheet's own
fragility. It is not: it is every sheet's, and frame 14's *"otherwise identical to frame 12, so the
panel is the only difference between them"* is the strictest instance on the sheet while being the one
that was not flagged.

**The first run bit on all four anchors, measured on the returned frames rather than argued.** Frames
12, 14 and 15 came back as **three different buildings** — a hipped terracotta roof over a colonnade
running right round, then a flat parapet over a four-column portico with steps, then a six-column
portico, the terracotta roof absent from the last two. The pedestal differs in every one of 2, 3, 4, 5,
6 and 11: two steps, an ashlar block, a low bench, a three-step pyramid, a rough-hewn rock, four steps.
The boundary stone is a knee-high cube in frame 7 and a flush marked flagstone in frame 10. The
counting slab is a two-by-eight grid in 16, a single row of five in 17 and a two-row slab in 18 — in
limestone in 16 and in **veined white marble** in 17, which this sheet forbids outright. And the
nineteen came back at **three different frame sizes** — 1536 by 1024, 1254 by 1254 and 1122 by 1402 —
against a rule of *"tile, do not scale"* at one frame size, so no sheet could be tiled straight.

**So the anchors are named, and every frame that cites one is generated from the anchor's returned
image rather than from its description.** **Frames 2, 7, 12 and 16 are the four anchors**: render them
first, judge them, then condition 3, 4, 5 and 11 on 2; 8, 9 and 10 on 7; 13 and 14 on 12; and 17 and 18
on 16. **Frames 3, 14 and 17 are anchors in turn** — 6 is conditioned on 3, 15 on 14, and 18's and 19's
counters on 17's. The anchor fixes the frame size and the camera as well as the object, because both are
part of *"identical"* and both are what make a tile legal.

**"Otherwise identical" is a claim about a conditioned render and is not available from a text
prompt.** Where a pair still does not read as one object in two states, **regenerate the dependent
against the anchor rather than loosening the claim** — and where no conditioning is available at all,
it is the claim that has to change and not the render, which is a finding for the owed list.

   composited frames has `#ECE4D3` as its most common colour, the exact pinned parchment covers at most
   **0.001 percent** of any frame, and their modal colours miss it by **5 to 86** units of RGB distance.
   A render has continuous shading, so sampling the set recovers eighteen near-misses rather than the hex
   the brief pinned. `test/crisisToneTables.test.ts` holds the table against `$ERA4` in both directions,
   recomputes every derived tone with the generator's own `MixHex`, and asserts that `#CCC8BD` is still a
   proposal — named on this sheet, and authored by no `$ERAn`.

   **The light is not there and does not need to be**: `crisis/succession.ts` already holds
   `SUCCESSION_LIGHT`, thirteen controls off §2.1 of the merged brief, so era four is the one of the
   three crises whose atmosphere was authored before its tones.
2. **What a plinth, a boundary stone and a counting slab measure.** Era one can say "about a quarter of
   the wall span" because `OVERSAIL = 1.24` exists. *"Knee height"*, *"low"* and *"22 percent down from
   the pedestal's hue"* are the brief's phrasing carried forward, and none is a number a renderer will
   honour.
3. **The proposal's opacity.** Frames 6 and 15 say *"a little over half"* and *"the dark end of the
   stone family"* because era one's `ghostWinterShield` ruled it so for a pale ground. Era four's stone
   is paler still and there is no succession ghost to read a value off.
4. **Whether the drift block's cut mark is legible at the shipped camera.** Frame 11 requires it to say
   **whose** block it is, which is a claim about a small groove at a distance and is not decidable
   without a renderer.
5. **How many districts there are, and whether there are exactly two claimants.** Each district needs a
   plinth, a boundary run and a mark, so the count multiplies three sheets; and the standing/seated
   pair separates two by silhouette, where a third claimant needs a third pose. Named as owed at
   `2026-08-01-succession-crisis-art-brief.md:380` and `:383`.
6. **RULED 2026-08-02: the shuttered building's corner marker is cut with the claimant's own mark.**
   Kept at its own number, closed rather than deleted, because frame 13 and era nine's owed item 10 both
   cite it. **What was asked**: frame 13 wants one cut mark because the crisis README specifies a
   one-glyph mark and does not say which glyph era four uses, era six's is ruled a horizontal bar and
   era four's was not ruled at all, and the first run — given *"a single mark"* and no shape — returned
   a serifed **I**, a character, so the gap produced an *illegal* frame under prohibition 5 rather than
   merely an arbitrary one. The question handed back was already sharpened: both legal shapes were
   spoken for, since the chevron and the ring **are the two claimants' marks**, so cutting either into a
   shuttered building's corner says *this district is claimed by that claimant*. **The ruling is that
   this is the intent.** A marker saying *who* claims a district is exactly what era four's crisis is
   about, so the glyph is the claimant's mark and **which mark appears is the content**. Frame 13 now
   carries it in executable form: the chevron in a chevron district, the ring in a ring district, no
   marker where the district is unclaimed, never a bar, never a character. **No third shape is wanted
   and prohibition 5 is untouched** — it supplies the two shapes rather than standing in the way.
7. **How many counters a batch seats.** The one-two-three ladder is a reading rather than a ruling
   (`:276`), which is why frames 17 and 18 draw *"four in one row"* and *"past the first row"*.
8. **ANSWERED 2026-08-02 by looking at all nineteen frames: the rewrite holds, and the clause that did
   not hold was the wrong clause to be leading with.** Applied from era two's ten building briefs, which
   produced usable images, and from era one's four measured failures, every one of which was a failure
   of ground. The subjects came back **in** their frames — nothing empty, nothing edge-jammed, nothing a
   section through terrain — so era four's set plus era six's and era nine's remain the case for putting
   the rule in `crisis-reference-packs/README.md` section 5.

   **The paving panel has quietly become the default, and that is now ruled on rather than deferred.**
   All **fourteen** frames sent straight onto the parchment came back with a panel under the object, so
   the *"no paving at all"* clause was obeyed zero times out of fourteen; **eleven** of those panels
   were bounded and cost nothing; the paving ran to all four frame edges in **four** frames; and the one
   frame that lost its subject was an unbounded one. The resolution is in **"When the subject is a piece
   of ground"**: the bounding requirement is promoted to the rule that blocks, and a bounded panel under
   an object that merely stands on the floor is tolerated, undescribed, and never given the height
   floor. **Not the lead's call any more, because the frames answered it.**

   **Two corrections to what was recorded here on 2026-08-02 before the frames were looked at.**
   *First*, the all-four-edges list was **four frames and not five**: `plinth-bare.png`,
   `plinth-standing-figure-proposed.png`, `counting-slab-empty.png` and `boundary-stone-both.png`.
   `counting-slab-one-row.png` is **bounded** — parchment is plainly visible at all four of its
   corners. Worth recording *how* that was settled, because two mechanical measures were tried and
   **both misclassified frames in both directions**: era four's paving and the parchment are within a
   few percent of each other in value, so a tone test reads pale paving as background; and a
   local-variance test reads a large flat flagstone face as background too, which is why
   `boundary-stone-both.png` scores as four clean corners while its paving fills the frame. Only
   looking at the nineteen images settles it. *Second*, **the unique-colour claim was the wrong way
   round.** The set does measure 7,450 to 63,340 — reproduced — but the four frames whose paving runs
   to all four edges are **four of the five lowest** counts in the set (7,450, 9,319, 10,567 and
   11,797, at ranks 1, 2, 3 and 5 of 19). A frame full of one flat material is *cheaper* in colours,
   not dearer, so the count is driven by how many materials and how much shading a frame holds and not
   by the paving. **That removes the one argument against tolerating a bounded panel.** Era two's
   flat-background band of 5,618 to 8,754 is left as recorded and is **not checkable in this tree** —
   `output/imagegen/era-02-neolithic-building-briefs/` holds a README and no images, and era two's
   generated art is untracked.

   **Still owed**: era six and era nine have never been run, so the bounding rule now written into all
   three sheets has been tested on one era's frames only.
9. **SETTLED 2026-08-02 at `#CCC8BD`, and this hue was PROPOSED here rather than looked up.** Said
   plainly, because it is the one thing a reader of this item most needs to know: **nothing in the tree
   authors a marble hue, so no line can be cited for it.** Searched before proposing — era four's style
   guide names marble in its materials table (`eras/era-04-classical/style-guide.md:108`) and gives it no
   hex; its palette table holds exactly two rows, body `#D2C8AA` and accent `#B4674A` (`:851-852`);
   `2026-07-30-era-palette-ladder.md` §5 authors one body hue and one accent per era and no material
   tones at all; the plate generator's `$ERA4` table has no marble entry
   (`tools/generate-winter-crisis-references.ps1:1907-1916`); and the pack README rules marble's
   *finish* — *"pale and faceted, never polished"* (`crisis-reference-packs/succession/README.md:84`) —
   which is a constraint on the surface and not a colour. **So this is a proposal for the lead to accept
   or replace, not a lookup.**

   **What was wrong.** Frame 16 read *"pale faceted marble in the era's stone `#D2C8AA`"* — **the
   limestone body hue exactly, a delta of (0, 0, 0)** — so the accent had no colour channel against the
   body it accents. **The first run shows what leaving it there cost**: frame 14's panel face came back
   `#B6A180` between the wall behind it at `#97866A` and a limestone column at `#ECDCC1`, inside the
   limestone family and legible as a distinct object only by its slab-and-bracket shape; and the three
   slab frames came back in **three different materials** — 16 the limestone-toned slab asked for, 17
   **veined white marble**, which frame 16's own *"never polished or veined"* forbids, and 18 a third,
   near-white slab with its counters standing proud instead of seated. Given nothing of its own to be,
   marble was invented three times and once illegally.

   **The derivation, which is chroma rather than lightness.** `#CCC8BD` holds the limestone's L\* (80.6
   against 80.7) and its hue angle (95.2° against 95.0°) and cuts its chroma from **16.3 to 6.0**. That
   is ΔE76 **10.33** from the limestone, **7.41** under `City_Art_Bible.md` §2's three-tone face model,
   against the pairwise floor of **6.72** the palette ladder derives for era four
   (`2026-07-30-era-palette-ladder.md` §5) — so it clears the bar the ladder uses for two eras' buildings
   standing side by side, which is a stricter test than an accent owes.

   **Why not simply whiter, which is the obvious answer and the wrong one.** Marble wants to go pale, and
   pale is the one direction closed: **frames 16 to 18 put the slab straight onto the parchment**, a slab
   lies flat, and the face the 49° camera mostly sees is its top face, which the three-tone model lifts
   26 percent. At `#D2C8AA` that top face is 0.840 against the parchment's 0.896, a step of **0.056**; a
   marble at L\* 85 would take it to 0.879 and leave **0.017**, so the slab would begin to dissolve into
   its own background. `#CCC8BD` holds the step at **0.057** — the value channel is not spent, so it
   stays available to the parchment, and the separation is bought on chroma where there is room.

   **Three bounds it was checked against, so a later reader does not have to re-derive them.**
   **Prohibition 2 does not stand in the way**: it forbids a *state* carried by hue — allegiance by
   tint — and a material hue for marble is not a state, so nothing about that ruling is touched.
   **Era six keeps its card**: its lead accent is *"the first genuinely cool note in the walk"*
   (`eras/era-06-early-modern/style-guide.md:819-820`), and `#CCC8BD` stays in era four's own warm
   quadrant at hue 95°, so it is a desaturated warm and not a cool note. **Era eight keeps its card**:
   *"goes neutral for the first time"* rests on its concrete at C\* 5.1, and marble at C\* 6.0 stays the
   more chromatic of the two — the two sit ΔE76 6.94 apart, which is named here rather than hidden, and
   the palette ladder is explicit that an accent is *"a trim on one building rather than a second
   building, so the pairwise floor is not the right instrument for it"* (`§5a`).

   **What is still owed on marble**: nothing about the hue, and the same thing as before about the
   render — whether a monolithic slab drained of its yellow reads as a *different material* rather than
   as a dirty limestone at the shipped camera. That is a renderer question and era four has no renderer.
