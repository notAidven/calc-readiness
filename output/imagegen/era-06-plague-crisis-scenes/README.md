## The propagation job: **reaching**

D-CRISIS-8 ruled the four propagation crises apart on deliberate axes so a player never meets the
same mental model twice, and `crisis-reference-packs/README.md:176-181` states that separation as a
fact about the **picture** rather than about the mechanics:

| Era | What propagates | Along what | What the player watches | The read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Hard Winter | Frost | Tile adjacency | An edge, and which way it is moving | **Creeping** |
| 4 Succession | Allegiance | Nothing spatial at all | An object arriving at a plinth | **Tipping** |
| **6 Plague** | **Infection** | **Authored links between buildings people use.** Two linked buildings need not touch | **A line in the floor, and what has travelled along it** | **Reaching** |
| 9 Intrusion | Compromise | Nothing visible | A table of readings, and how old each one is | **Suspecting** |

**Four different visual jobs**: watch an edge, watch an object appear, watch a line, read a board.
`README.md:183-186` says nothing in this pass may blur them, so **a shortening pass that made the
three crisis sheets rhyme would destroy exactly what that ruling protects.** Era six's row, argued in
era six's own terms:

- **Against the frost, and it is the sharpest test in the set.** *"Era six's board has lines on it and
  era one's has none"* (`2026-08-01-plague-crisis-art-brief.md:252`). Frost moves through tile
  adjacency, so the question is *how far has the front got* and the answer is an **area**; era six
  moves through drawn lines, so the question is *what is connected to what* and the answer is a
  **graph**. The consequence inverts: **in era one a building is safer for being far from the edge,
  and in era six distance means nothing at all.**
- **Against era four.** Era four draws no connections whatever — its boundary stones **enclose**
  rather than **join**. **Era four's board is a set of regions and era six's is a set of edges.**
- **Against era nine.** Both hide something and they hide different things. **Era six hides what has
  travelled along a visible network; era nine hides the status of a known thing and has no network at
  all.** Held to: *read the graph and cut it* against *read the board and pay to know more* (`:265`).
- **One sentence for a review:** *era six is the crisis where the player can see every path and not
  what has walked down it.*

**The links are visible and the infection travelling along them is not**, which is how the lead's
*"partly hidden"* and the implementation's *"visible"* are made to agree. **Links are drawn in full
from the first turn**: no fog, no discovery, no unlocking. Infection markers appear only when a cycle
resolves.

**Frames 4, 5 and 2 carry the propagation.** Frame 4 is a link that is open, frame 5 a link that has
carried, and **frame 2 is the topology itself**: one notched band crossing the island between two
buildings that do not touch, with two untouched buildings standing between them. The brief calls that
frame *"that sentence as a picture"* for *distance means nothing at all*. **Render 4, 5 and 2 first**;
if frame 2 does not read, nothing else on the sheet can carry the crisis.

### Two collisions, and both bind this sheet

**1. Era six against era seven, which costs the most to get wrong.** D-CRISIS-8 moved the Plague onto
a link layer the Boiler Cascade also wants, so the two are **closer in machinery and further apart in
semantics**. The Plague's edges are a hazard the player wants to **CUT**; the Cascade's are a supply
line the player wants **INTACT**. An identical visual language would invite a player to sever era
seven's network, **which is the correct move in era six and the destructive one in era seven**
(`crisis-reference-packs/README.md:190-195`, `2026-08-01-plague-crisis-art-brief.md:206`).

> **Era six's links are IN the floor. Era seven's are ON it.**

Era six inlays a line in the paving: flat, walkable, part of the ground. Era seven runs a pipe on
sleepers: raised, structural, something you would not step on. **One is a path people take; the other
is a thing that feeds something.** **Frames 10 and 11 draw both**, which is why a frame about era
seven is on an era-six sheet. Era seven is shelved and the pair is still worth rendering: D-CRISIS-32
defers rather than cancels, and the cheapest moment to fix this is before either is built.

**2. Era six against era nine, and this half binds era six.** **Era six must never be given a
confidence grade.** A hedged reading is era nine's, protected by D-CRISIS-22, and giving era six one
would collapse the two crises into one (`:237`). **There is no "probably infected" state on this
sheet**: a band is open, suspended or carrying, and a building is marked or it is not. *(The
reciprocal half — that era nine must never be given an edge layer — is on era nine's sheet, where it
binds.)*

## The shared direction

*Read once. Every brief below inherits all of it, and none of them restates it.*

- **One object, isolated on plain parchment `#ECE4D3` and contained within its own footprint.**
  Isometric low-poly, one fixed camera tilted about 49 degrees down, no horizon tilt, framed 1400 by
  900. **The parchment is a background rather than a surface**: the object stands on it, nothing is
  drawn on it, and nothing stands beyond it. There is no ground, no horizon and no sky. Parchment is
  the Art Bible's own ground tone (`docs/Game_Visual_Design_Brief.md:157`), so an object sits on it
  the way it will sit on the island.
- **Northern Early Modern town drawn to a measured drawing, five materials.** **Pale dressed stone
  at the base, openings and structural corners frames the smooth work above** — broad channelled
  blocks at ground level, everything above them flat and calm, separated by a string course;
  warm buff lime plaster as the dominant body `#C7A48E`, flat and faintly chalky, an applied finish
  with nothing to hide and never divided by visible studwork; localized hand-made muted red-brown
  brick `#B1775A`, never a dominant façade; **dark slate and sheet lead on domes, cupolas and roofs
  `#596675`, laid in rolled bays with the seams running with the curve so a dome reads as tapering
  panels rather than a smooth shell**, the one cool note above ground level; and glass in wooden or
  leaded casements, panes small and held in bars, **the glazing grid reading at zoom-out, which is
  what makes a wall look counted**. No terracotta roof carries over from era four. Pilasters at
  constant spacing and arcaded loggias of round-headed arches are the ornament.
- **Three tone steps per surface under one upper-left key** — top face lightened about 26 percent, the
  hue on the left, the right darkened about 28. **No runtime lights**: no cast shadow, no ambient
  occlusion, no specular highlight, no rim light, no gradient. Never describe an object by light
  falling on it.
- **The subject fills the frame**, centred, its silhouette at least two thirds of the frame's height,
  nothing clipped. **Height is measured on the built object only**: a dome's lantern counts, a plume
  or a ghost above it does not. *(That clause is
  #411's and is the one part of it the
  parchment does not retire — era one's pass three returned a hearth about 100 pixels tall under a
  smoke column carrying the total to 682, satisfying the number and missing the point.)*
- **One state per frame**, so the sheet stays an asset set rather than a diagram. Every comparison is
  composited afterwards from the single renders, never asked for in a prompt.
- **Every side must read**, because the camera orbits a full 360°, and **the frame carries no label**
  — no caption, legend, annotation, leader line, arrow, numeral or text. The reading key is this
  document.

**Absent on purpose, because each is a later era's signature move:** structural iron and machine-made
brick, **soot as a finish** (which is why this era's stucco stays clean even where it is old),
reinforced concrete and the cantilever, and the unitised curtain wall. **And era five's exposed timber
frame is retired here**, the one signature the run takes away rather than adds, so no wall is divided
into panels by visible studs.

### The seven rulings a picture can break

**The rule that cut 68 fed clauses to seven: a prohibition survives only if breaking it produces a
picture that is wrong about the DESIGN rather than one that is merely ugly.** Everything the parchment
retired went with the ground it was about — the floating slab, the detached tile, the void behind the
floor — because there is no ground left in the frame to get wrong except the panels the ground section
below governs. The generic ones went on era one's measured ground that its failed run held essentially
all of them without being asked. **What replaced the rest is a change of grammar**: era two's briefs
carry **zero** *"no X"* clauses across all ten briefs, measured, and separate a confusable pair by naming the
axis instead, which is what every *"must not resemble"* below does. **Feed these seven verbatim, last,
and add nothing.**

> - **A link is inlaid flush into the paving**, level with the slabs, part of the ground. Never
>   raised, never on sleepers, never cylindrical, never directional, and never drawn in the
>   dark slate/lead `#596675`.
> - **A door mark is a single horizontal cut bar.** That is the only mark on any door, and it is cut
>   rather than chalked or painted.
> - **Every band is the same width, and nothing travels visibly along one.** Nothing in the frame
>   invites the player to reason about how much traffic a link carries.
> - **A band is open, suspended or carrying, and a building is marked or it is not. Those are all the
>   states there are, and nothing sits between them.**
> - **Every outline encloses and none fills.** Whatever is inside an outline is drawn exactly as it
>   would be outside it.
> - **Everything is clean, maintained and intact, under a pale even midday.** The town has stopped,
>   not decayed.
> - **No mastery gold `#FFB703`.**

**Fed to every frame except 11 — and that exception is named here rather than left to whoever is
holding the prompt.** **Frame 11 is not an era-six frame**: it draws era seven's link on purpose, so a
reviewer can see that era six's is not it. Rulings 1 and 6 are statements about era six, and fed to
frame 11 verbatim they forbid its whole subject. Ruling 1 forbids *"raised, on sleepers, cylindrical,
directional"*, which is **every property frame 11 exists to draw**; ruling 6's *"clean, maintained and
intact"* forbids the soot-darkened brick that is era seven's finish and that the shared direction
already lists as absent from era six. **For frame 11 those two are fed as the contrast instead**: ruling
1 describes frame 10, and frame 11 is its exact negative, which is the entire point of the pair. The
other five are fed to frame 11 unchanged. **Nothing is weakened and nothing is dropped by this** — all
seven still bind the fourteen frames that are era six's, and the one frame that is not era six is the
one frame two of them are not statements about.

**Why each survived, for the reviewer rather than the generator.** **The inlay** is the era's central
ruling and the era-seven collision — an identical visual language would invite a player to sever era
seven's supply network, and the grey-blue exclusion is part of it, because grey-blue against pale
dressed stone reads as a thing lying on the floor (`README.md:190-195`). **The bar** is ruled because
it is *"unambiguous and unclaimed"* (`:349`), and the plague cross is the single most likely thing a
generator will reach for given the subject: the largest gap on this sheet between how obvious the
wrong answer is and how wrong it is. **Traffic volume** is the rule the brief says in terms an artist
breaks without touching a line of code (`:194`); stated as *"every band is the same width"* it is
enforceable in one glance, where the old form enumerated six ways to break it. **The closed state
set** is how the hedge ruling is best carried: a confidence grade is era nine's, reserved by
D-CRISIS-22 (`:237`), and naming every legal state outlaws every partial one by construction rather
than by listing them. **Enclose-not-fill** keeps the town from becoming a map, which is what frame 3's
whole subject turns on. **Clean and intact** is the atmosphere: the gardeners are still alive, and the
immaculate maintenance is what makes the emptiness read as a decision rather than as abandonment.
**Mastery gold** is reserved outright across every era.

### When the subject is a piece of ground

**This is the section era six turns on.** Seven of its fifteen subjects are things *in a floor* —
frames 4 and 5 are an inlaid band, 6 to 9 are a stone laid across one, and 10 is a band between two
buildings — and *"flush"* is half of the era-seven collision. **You cannot show a band inlaid flush
without showing what it is inlaid in.** So *"no ground in the picture"* and *"the subject is a piece
of ground"* have to be reconciled here, and getting it wrong is how the floating slab came back four
times on era one.

**A piece of ground is drawn as a made object, never as a piece of terrain.** A paved panel is a
manufactured thing — cut units, fitted joints, a dressed outer edge — and it goes on the parchment as
any other object does: complete, centred, within its own footprint.

**The rule that blocks a frame is that the panel is BOUNDED, and 2026-08-02 promoted it to that
position on the evidence of era four's nineteen returned frames.** It used to sit behind *"only a mark
that is IN the floor gets a panel"*, which is a rule about **whether** a panel may appear, and era
four's run settles that the wrong half was leading. Measured there by looking at every returned image:
**fourteen frames are sent straight onto the parchment and fourteen of fourteen came back standing on a
paving panel**, so the *"no paving at all"* clause was obeyed **zero times**; **eleven** of those
panels were bounded and cost nothing; the paving ran to **all four frame edges in four** frames; and
**the one frame that lost its subject was an unbounded one** — `boundary-stone-both.png` returned its
boundary stone as a flagstone lying flush in a floor that fills the frame, with no block at all. **The
defect tracks unboundedness rather than the panel's presence.**

> **The parchment is visible on all four sides of everything in the frame, and no paving ever touches a
> frame edge.** Whatever is in shot — object, panel, or an object on a panel — sits complete inside the
> frame with plain parchment round it.

**So a bounded panel under something that merely stands on the floor is tolerated rather than
requested** — under a building on its rusticated base, under a covered vessel on its pedestal. It is
not a defect and not worth a regeneration. **But it is never described here and never conditioned on**,
and it never carries the height floor, because a panel the brief did not ask for is scenery the
generator added and describing it makes it a second subject. **Only a mark that is IN the floor gets a
panel that is part of the SUBJECT** — described, conditioned on its anchor, and measured — and on this
sheet that is seven of the fifteen, which is why this section is the longest here.

**The panel and the slab separate on the edge, and that is the whole of it.** A made panel ends where
a paving unit ends: its outer edge is a **dressed square arris in the same stone as the top face, one
course thick**, finished the way a kerb stone is, and the panel lies flat on the parchment with
nothing beneath it. A floating slab ends in **a section through the ground**: a thick body with an
underside, a torn or tapered edge, soil or rock below the paving, and parchment visible under its far
side. The first is masonry, the second is landscape lifted out — and the second is what came back four
times on era one.

**A third edge came back on era four that neither of those describes, and it is the parchment's own
doing.** Three of its panel frames ended in **a torn, deckled sheet edge** — paving on a ragged
rectangle of textured paper — so the parchment stopped being a background and became a *sheet* lying in
the picture. **A panel's edge is masonry**: dressed, square, one course thick, and never torn, deckled,
ragged, feathered or vignetted. **This sheet is the one most exposed to it**, because a flush inlaid
band already asks a generator to draw a rectangle of floor and nothing else.

**So describe a panel by its material, never by its size.** *"Only a few cells of ground at most"* was
tried on era one and read as permission to draw ground with a visible edge, which is the slab; that
phrasing is deleted and must not come back. Say the panel is large dressed slabs `#CFCDC4` over a
deeper cut `#BAB8AE` in a deliberate geometric pattern with setting-out lines cut into the stone, and
let its extent follow from the band running through it.

**Frames 10 and 11 use the identical panel**, and that is not a convenience. The pair's entire claim
is a difference between what sits **in** the floor and what sits **on** it, so if the two panels differ
the comparison has a second variable in it and settles nothing.

**Which object carries the two-thirds height floor, because on this sheet it is never obvious.** The
floor is measured *"on the built object only"*, and a frame holding both a panel and something on it has
two candidates with no rule for choosing — which is how era one's pass three satisfied a height floor
with a smoke column instead of a hearth. **The rule is that the two thirds is carried by whichever
object the frame's countable mark sits on.** For frames 4 and 5 that mark is the floor itself: an inlaid
band is flush and has no height at all, so **the panel is the built object and carries the two thirds**,
with the band running corner to corner across it so its length has room for the notches to stay
countable. For frames 6 to 9 the countable mark is a count of raised bands round a stone standing proud
of the floor, so **the stone carries the two thirds** and the panel stays under it. For 10 and 11 the
two buildings carry it. **Era four's first run is the measurement for what leaving this open costs**: the
panel was the taller object in all four of its boundary-stone frames, and in `boundary-stone-unmarked.png`
it took about 90 percent of frame height against about 37 for the stone the countable mark sits on.

### Every sheet here needs one object twice, so each needs an anchor

**Every *"the band and panel of frame 4"*, *"the stone, band and panel of frame 6"*, *"the same panel as
frame 10"* and *"the building of frame 12"* is an identity requirement across two independent
generations, and two text prompts do not return the same object.** Frame 13's *"otherwise identical to
frame 12, so the pedestal is the only difference between them"* is the strictest instance on the sheet.
Owed item 9 records this for the band counts; it is not the band counts' alone.

**Era four's first run is the measurement, since era six has never been run.** All four of that sheet's
anchors diverged: three frames of one building came back as three different buildings, six frames of one
pedestal as six different pedestals, four frames of one boundary stone at four different heights and
camera distances, and one counting slab as three different slabs in two different materials. The
nineteen also came back at three different frame sizes, against the same *"tile, do not scale"* rule this
section carries.

**So the anchors are named, and every dependent is generated from the anchor's returned image rather than
from its description.** **Frames 4, 10 and 12 are the anchors.** Render 4 first and condition 5 and 6 on
it; condition 7, 8 and 9 on the **returned frame 6**, so that the stone and frame 4's panel and band both
carry through the whole barrier run; condition 11 on 10, which is what *"frames 10 and 11 use the
identical panel"* actually requires of a production step; and condition 13, 14 and 15 on 12. The anchor
fixes the frame size and the camera as well as the object, because both are part of *"identical"* and
both are what make a tile legal. **"Otherwise identical" is a claim about a conditioned render and is not
available from a text prompt.**

## The run sheet: `imagegen-run-sheet.jsonl`, fifteen jobs in four passes

**Executable straight through, top to bottom, and the anchor structure is in the file rather than in its
ordering.** Every line carries `frame`, `step`, `pass`, `role`, `anchor` and `input` beside era four's own
`out`, `prompt` and `size`, so a variant names the anchor it derives from and gives the absolute path of
the image to condition on. Ordering alone would have left that implicit, and implicit is how era four's
first run came back as three different buildings.

| Pass | Steps | Frames | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `1-anchors` | 1-3 | **4, 10, 12** | Text-to-image. **Judge all three before spending anything on pass 2** |
| `2-variants-of-the-anchors` | 4-9 | 5, 6 ← 4; 11 ← 10; 13, 14, 15 ← 12 | Image-to-image |
| `3-variants-of-frame-6` | 10-12 | 7, 8, 9 ← **6** | Image-to-image, off a returned variant |
| `4-scene-frames` | 13-15 | **2**, then 1, then 3 | Text-to-image. No anchor, and the parchment rule is suspended |

**Nine of the fifteen are image-to-image and that is not a preference.** Frame 6 is a variant in pass 2
and an anchor in pass 3, which is why 7, 8 and 9 wait: conditioning them on frame 4 would give four
barrier stones instead of one stone at four band counts, and the countability claim
`closure-duration-bands.png` exists to test would become a claim about four different objects.

**Every variant prompt carries the same two paragraphs**, and the second of them is a defect era four's
anchored pass actually produced. The first pins the anchor's camera, scale, crop and margins. The second
says *do not re-fit the camera to a taller subject*: `#455` records that an anchored variant re-fitted its
camera to what it had added, **so the pedestal read smaller in the variant than in the anchor** and the
pair could not be tiled as one object in two states. A variant holds the anchor's framing, not the
subject's — what it adds extends into the margin the anchor already has.

**Order within the passes is this sheet's own two orderings honoured together.** *"Render 4, 5 and 2
first"* ranks what has to be judged early; *"render 1, 2 and 3 last"* is about where the residual risk is.
Both hold: frames 4 and 5 are steps 1 and 4, so the propagation read is judged before eleven frames are
spent, and frame 2 leads the scene pass rather than the sheet.

**Every frame is 1248x1248.** That is era four's delivered size, chosen because the comparison sheets tile
without scaling and because **1254 is not a multiple of sixteen**, which the generator needs. The shared
direction's *"framed 1400 by 900"* is not a legal size on either axis, and era four's first run — three
different frame sizes across nineteen frames, so that no sheet could be tiled at all — is what a set with
no single pinned size produces. *(Era four's one scene frame is 1536x1024 and was deliberately left there
when the other eighteen were unified, since a scene frame is in no comparison sheet. Frames 1, 2 and 3
could take that landscape frame on the same reasoning; they are written at 1248x1248 because the
instruction was one size unless the sheets say otherwise, and it is a three-line edit if the lead prefers
the wider frame for an island-scale span.)*

**The seven rulings are fed verbatim and last in every prompt**, and frame 11's prompt carries the
substitution this document already rules: rulings 1 and 6 arrive as the contrast, describing frame 10,
because fed as instructions they forbid frame 11's whole subject. The other five are unchanged.

**Where this sheet says *"matte"*, *"painted timber"* or *"a covered vessel"*, the run sheet says a hex**,
because `apps/web/src/city/three/plague-tones.ts` now pins one: the shut inlay is `#7B7A73`, a notch
`#66645F`, the shutters `#7C6A55`, the door panel `#596675` with its cut bar reading `#ACB2BA`, the

### 3. A proposed closure, and the set it separates — `proposed-closure-and-the-set-it-separates.png`

**A scene frame, and kept whole because splitting it destroys it.** An outline round a group of
buildings only means anything with the group inside it and the rest of the town outside; the proposal
only means anything beside the committed stone it is not yet. Both are facts about one frame.

**Silhouette.** A paved square with five warm-buff plaster buildings and a network of inlaid bands between them,
showing one closure being considered rather than made, and the group of buildings it would cut off.

**Materials.** Five warm-buff plaster buildings `#C7A48E` on pale rusticated dressed-stone bases,
with localized muted brick, on geometric paving `#CFCDC4`
over `#BAB8AE`, joined by narrow bands inlaid flush in a darker cut of the floor `#9A9890`, each running
threshold to threshold.

**The three marks.** One band carries a **committed barrier stone**: a solid plain dressed block
`#ACAAA0` laid across it with two raised bands cut round it, opaque, **darker than every tone in the
paving it lies on** (owed item 11). A second carries a **proposed** one
instead: **the same stone whole, block and both raised bands together, rendered see-through at a little
over half opacity**, the band and paving legible through it and never hidden behind it, made of the
**dark end of the stone family rather than the pale end** so it stays visible against the pale floor.
The band it would cut is **outlined along its whole length** with a thin raised edge each side rather
than being notched or recessed. Behind the proposal, **two of the five buildings are enclosed together
by a second, lighter raised outline** drawn on the paving around both of them and the band between them,
**following the paving's own joint lines and not filling the area inside it**.

**The word *outline* is doing two jobs in that paragraph and only one of them keeps it.** Both outlines
above are **enclosures** — a boundary along a band and a boundary round a group of buildings — and an
outline is the right grammar for those, which is what prohibition 5 rules. **A proposal is not an
enclosure**, and until 2026-08-02 this frame drew it as one: *"a raised outline of the same stone's
shape, hollow"*. That differs from the committed stone in **fill** rather than in translucency, so the
two cannot read as one object in two states; a raised stone frame round an empty middle is committed
masonry rather than a proposal; and it sat one dash-pattern from the *"no dashed line, dotted line"*
apparatus this frame's own prohibition forbids. **Era four's first run settles it against the
outline**: given exactly that wording it returned a solid dark raised moulding with its own shading, in
a shape that was not the proposed object's, where the same sheet's see-through ghost came back
see-through (`era-04-succession-crisis-scenes/README.md`, "One proposal grammar"). **So a proposal here
is the stone object itself, whole, rendered see-through — never an outline and never hollow.**

**What must read.** One stone has been placed and one has only been proposed, and the two are obviously
the same object in two states. The lighter outline reads as **enclosing a group of buildings** rather
than as another link between them.

**What it must not resemble.** **A minimap**, which is what an outline round a region becomes the moment
anything inside it changes. They separate on **enclosure against fill**: the outline is a raised edge in
the paving and everything inside it is exactly as it is outside — nothing tinted, shaded, dimmed or
greyed. And, as frame 6 of era four's sheet, the proposal must not resemble a **UI overlay**: no dashed
line, dotted line, hatching, crosshair, reticle, cursor, arrow or bracket. **These are stone objects and
their proposals.**

**Shape from** the barrier stone and its bands in `plague/05-closure-duration-bands.svg`, the band
network and buildings in `plague/03-long-link-not-adjacency.svg`, and the proposal treatment from
`hard-winter/09-building-protection-states.svg`, third mass, which is era one's ghosted shield. **No

primitive at all.

   discovered in returned art. The fifteen frames themselves have never been run, so this is a
   correction to their specification. **Era four is untouched**: `#B4674A` remains its own guide's
   accent and `succession-tones.ts`'s, and era six simply holds no copy.
1. **Every hex value on this sheet, and they are not all sourced alike.** **Four are the style
   guide's** — body `#C7A48E`, localized brick `#B1775A`, pale trim `#D8CDB3` and
   slate/sheet-lead accent `#596675`. **The
   whole ground family is the plate generator's alone** — `#CFCDC4`, `#BAB8AE`, `#9D9B92` and `#9A9890`
   from the `$ERA6` table (`tools/generate-winter-crisis-references.ps1`), with no line in the style
   guide behind any of them. **And `#ACAAA0` is this sheet's own**, proposed at owed item 11 and sourced
   nowhere else, which is why it is called out here rather than left in a list.

   **SETTLED 2026-08-03: `apps/web/src/city/three/plague-tones.ts` now exists** and pins all of them,
   plus the six the frame briefs described in words and never in hex — the shut inlay `#7B7A73`, a notch
   `#66645F`, the shutters `#7C6A55` and their shaded face `#615342`, the door panel `#596675` with its
   cut bar `#ACB2BA`, the threshold pedestal `#CFCDC4` and the covered vessel's body `#6F6256`. Each
   entry says which of four things it is: the style guide, `$ERA6`, a literal one of the generator's
   crisis primitives draws, or a documented `Darken`/`Lighten` of one of those.
   `test/crisisToneTables.test.ts` censuses every `$ERA6` role in both directions and recomputes every
   derived tone with the generator's own `MixHex`, so a value that drifts from the thing that drew the
   plates fails rather than merely being regrettable. **What is still owed is the atmosphere preset**:
   `crises/era-06-plague.md` §2.1 is eleven rows of direction and no numbers, and a `CrisisAtmosphere`
   wants thirteen values, so a `PLAGUE_LIGHT` would be eleven invented ones. The one control the brief
   treats as identifying, `key.shadowIntensity`, is given as a bound — *below era one's 0.55* — rather
   than a value.
2. **How deep an inlay is, and how a flush band reads at the shipped camera.** The whole era-six against
   era-seven separation rests on *flush* against *raised*, and **flush is the harder half to draw**: a
   band level with the paving may vanish at the resting camera or may read as a painted stripe. There is
   no geometry to measure and it is not decidable in prose. **The parchment rewrite raises the stakes
   here rather than lowering them**, because the band now has to read as inlay against a panel rather
   than against a whole floor.
3. **What the covered vessel is.** The brief offers *"a vinegar vessel or a smoke pot at a door"* and
   does not pick one. Frames 1, 2, 13 and 15 all say "a covered vessel" and leave the object unresolved.
4. **Whether one band, two bands and a collar are countable at the shipped camera.** Frames 6 to 9 draw
   the ladder and the brief notes a rung above three cycles would stop being countable. **Which rung
   stops being countable is a renderer question**, and era six has no renderer.
5. **What a barrier stone measures.** *"Low"*, *"squared"* and *"knee height"* are the brief's phrasing
   carried forward and none is a number a build will honour.
6. **Whether duration is what the band count sizes, and how long each rung holds.** The ruled cadence
   says *"permission or duration"* and does not choose; one cycle, two cycles and until the outbreak
   ends is a reading rather than a ruling. Frames 6 to 9 draw the rungs without saying what they buy.
7. **Whether some links are unknown until used.** Carried as owed in the brief, and it would change
   every frame here, because an unknown link cannot be an inlaid band and an inlaid band is permanent
   stonework.
8. **How many links there are.** Every link is a band and a hit target, so the count multiplies three
   sheets. Belongs to whoever authors era six's land.
9. **Whether one band and two bands survive being tiled rather than drawn together.** The pre-`#410`
   version drew them in one frame where a generator could keep them consistent by construction; tiled
   from separate renders they may not be. **That is a real risk the restructure introduced and this
   rewrite does not remove.** If the composited sheet fails its countability claim, the remedy is a
   paired render rather than a looser claim.
10. **Whether the object-on-parchment rewrite holds, and it is least certain here.** Era six is the sheet
    with the most ground subjects, so it is the one where the panel rule is doing the most work and the
    one likeliest to produce a slab. **Era six has never been run.** If frames 4, 5 and 10 come back with
    a made panel rather than a lifted piece of terrain, that is the strongest single piece of evidence
    the rule will get.

    **Era four's run of the same rule was read frame by frame on 2026-08-02, and it changed which half of
    the rule this sheet leads with** (`#431`). Its subjects came back in their frames and none was a
    section through terrain; **the panel became the default** — fourteen frames sent to bare parchment,
    fourteen panels; **eleven of those panels were bounded and cost nothing**; **four frames ran paving
    to all four edges**; and **the one frame that lost its subject was unbounded**. So expect a panel
    under everything here rather than a bare field, and **judge it on whether the parchment survives on
    all four sides** rather than on whether the panel is there at all. That is now written into "When
    the subject is a piece of ground" above.

    **What is genuinely still open for era six, and it is not the same question.** Era four's fourteen
    tolerated panels were all under objects that merely *stand* on a floor, so its run says nothing about
    the case this sheet turns on: **a panel whose own flushness is the subject.** Frames 4, 5 and 10 need
    the panel to read as inlaid-in rather than lying-on, and era four never asked a panel to carry a
    claim. The bounding rule protects the frame; it does not settle *flush*, which is owed item 2.
11. **SETTLED 2026-08-02: the barrier stone is `#ACAAA0`. The direction was looked up; the value was
    PROPOSED here.** Both halves stated separately, because they carry different weight.

    **What was wrong.** The stone was `#C8C3B6` and the floor it lies on is `#CFCDC4` over a deeper cut
    `#BAB8AE`, so **the stone's luminance, 0.765, fell between the paving's two at 0.803 and 0.720**:
    bracketed by the floor, with no value channel against it at all. What separated it was its faces,
    its standing proud, and a step of 0.170 against the inlay band `#9A9890` it is laid across. A
    barrier stone that reads as one more paving unit is the failure the whole closure ladder rests on.

    **The direction is authored, and this is the part that is a lookup.** Era six's style guide rules
    this exact problem, twice. *"The rusticated base is the answer and it is already in §2 … heavy,
    deeply channelled stone at the bottom of every wall gives each building a **dark banded plinth**
    exactly where it meets the floor — a shadow line the flat lighting model would not otherwise
    produce"* (`eras/era-06-early-modern/style-guide.md:776-779`); and, generalised into a rule, **"the
    durable resolution is that each pale era carries its own dark element at ground level — era two's
    undressed-stone footings, era four's kerb and coping, era six's rusticated base — rather than
    relying on the floor. Recorded here because era six is where it stops being a coincidence and
    becomes a rule"** (`:793-796`). So the era already says a piece of stone that must read against a
    pale floor goes **dark**, and says the floor stays *"cooler and greyer than the buildings"* (`:780`).
    **A barrier stone is that dark ground element, brought out of the wall base and laid across a link.**

    **The value is not authored anywhere, so it is proposed.** Era six's style guide holds the
    body `#C7A48E`, localized brick `#B1775A`, pale trim `#D8CDB3` and accent `#596675`,
    plus §1.1's shared base;
    `2026-07-30-era-palette-ladder.md` §5 authors one body and one accent per era and no material tones;
    the whole ground family lives only in the plate generator's `$ERA6` table
    (`tools/generate-winter-crisis-references.ps1`), and `#C8C3B6` was a function default
    (`ps1:1160`). **No line can be cited for a barrier-stone hue.**

    **`#ACAAA0` is the one empty rung of the era's own ground family**, and that is where it comes from
    rather than from taste: the authored ground tones run 0.803 (slabs), 0.720 (deeper cut), 0.607
    (kerb), 0.595 (inlay band), leaving a **0.113 gap between the deeper cut and the kerb with nothing
    in it**, and `#ACAAA0` is the exact midpoint of those two authored values. It is L\* 69.5, C\* 5.5,
    hue 101.6° — inside the family's own band on both (C\* 4.5-5.4, hue 99.7-101.6°), so it invents a
    rung and not a colour.

    **What it buys, measured.** 0.138 below the slabs, 0.055 below the deeper cut, so **the stone is
    darker than every tone in the panel it lies on** where it used to sit between two of them — which
    is the defect as stated, and the sign flip rather than the margin is the fix. The larger of the two
    steps, 0.138 against the slab face, is the one that matters most, because the deeper cut shows in
    the joints and the slab face is the field.

    **What it costs, named rather than buried.** The step against the shut inlay band drops from 0.170
    to **0.070**, because the gap the stone has to fit into is bounded by the band below it as well as
    by the floor above. Three things carry that pair regardless: the stone stands proud and shows three
    shaded faces where the band is flush, frames 6 to 9 draw the crossed band **matte and recessed**
    along its length, and the stone's raised bands are still cut 46 percent darker at `#5D5C56` — a step
    of 0.306 below the stone — so the countability the closure ladder rests on is untouched.

    **Two directions were closed and it is worth saying why.** *Paler* inverts the era's own authored
    rule and runs at the parchment, which is 0.896 and is what a panel lies on. *Darker still* runs at
    the thing era six forbids outright: *"era six's paving is washed, its balustrade is pale, and its
    joints are not black"* (`:754-757`), and dirt is era seven's completely. `#ACAAA0` is **lighter than
    the era's own kerb** at L\* 69.5 against 63.9, so it draws no value the era does not already draw at
    ground level, and it remains visibly distinct from the dark slate/lead accent forbidden by
    ruling 1 on a link band.

    **No ruling is touched.** Era six's closed state set — *"a band is open, suspended or carrying"* —
    is carried by band count and by the inlay going matte, exactly as before; a **material** tone for
    the stone is not a state, and this era has no prohibition against hue in any case.

    **One thing still owed, and the other half closed on 2026-08-03.** The plates draw `#C8C3B6`
    (`plague/05-closure-duration-bands.svg`), and this sheet cites plates **for shape only**, so the
    divergence is deliberate — but bringing `$ERA6` and the plates into line **on the barrier stone**
    is a code change and a regeneration, outside a documentation pass, and it is still owed. *(Item 0's
    pass regenerated the six plates for the BODY and roof hues and nothing else, so they are current
    in warm buff and dark slate/lead and still draw the generator's default stone.)* **The value is now pinned**:
    `plague-tones.ts` holds `BARRIER_STONE` at `#ACAAA0` with the derivation above, and
    `test/crisisToneTables.test.ts` asserts it in both directions — that the run sheet still feeds it,
    and that no `$ERAn` has started authoring it, since a palette that adopted the value would make
    this a lookup rather than the proposal it is documented as.
12. **NEW 2026-08-03: what opacity a proposal is drawn at. A gap this sheet had rather than an answer
    it lost.** Frame 3 asks for the committed stone *"rendered see-through at a little over half
    opacity"* in *"the dark end of the stone family"*. **Both halves are era four's**, borrowed with the
    proposal grammar and never carried into this sheet's own owed list — era four's item 3 records that
    the wording came from era one's `ghostWinterShield`, which ruled it for era ONE's pale ground, and
    that *"there is no succession ghost to read a value off"*. There is no plague ghost either.

    **The tones pass does not settle it and could not**: an opacity is not a hue. It is also the one
    open question here with a shipped precedent saying the arithmetic will be wrong —
    `winterFireGhost.ts` had to raise its ramp off a photograph because *"a translucent wash over
    near-white snow composites to a pale tint whatever the arithmetic says"*, and era six's floor is
    0.803 on this sheet's own convention, which is that case again. **What the tone table adds is
    somewhere for the answer to go**: a ruled proposal tone is a named entry beside `BARRIER_STONE`
    rather than a phrase in a prompt. **Team lead, and it binds era nine's frame 14 identically.**
